# Insurance Glossary & Self-Service Resources

## Insurance Terms Glossary

### Core Insurance Concepts

**Actual Cash Value (ACV)**: Replacement cost minus depreciation. Used to value property at time of loss.

**Aggregate Limit**: Maximum amount insurer will pay for all claims during policy period (typically 12 months), regardless of number of claims.

**All-Risk Coverage**: Policy covering all perils except those specifically excluded (opposite of named perils).

**Binder**: Temporary insurance contract providing immediate coverage while formal policy is prepared.

**Blanket Coverage**: Single limit applying to multiple locations, items, or types of property.

**Broker**: Independent insurance advisor representing client's interests (not the insurance carrier).

**Carrier**: Insurance company providing coverage and assuming risk.

**Certificate of Insurance (COI)**: Document evidencing insurance coverage, typically required by vendors/contracts.

**Claim**: Formal request to insurance carrier for payment under policy terms.

**Claims-Made Policy**: Covers claims made during policy period, regardless of when incident occurred (requires continuous coverage or tail).

**Coinsurance**: Percentage sharing of costs between insured and insurer (e.g., 80/20 split) OR penalty for underinsuring property.

**Deductible**: Amount insured pays before insurance coverage applies (per occurrence or aggregate).

**Endorsement**: Amendment to insurance policy adding, removing, or modifying coverage.

**Exclusion**: Specific circumstance, peril, or type of loss not covered by policy.

**Excess Liability**: Additional liability coverage above underlying primary policies.

**First Notice of Loss (FNOL)**: Initial report of claim to insurance carrier.

**Indemnity**: Compensation for loss or damage; restoring insured to pre-loss financial position.

**Insured vs. Additional Insured**: Insured is named party with coverage; additional insured has coverage extended under someone else's policy.

**Liability**: Legal responsibility for injury or damage to others.

**Limit**: Maximum amount insurer will pay for covered loss.

**Loss Payee**: Entity with financial interest in insured property (e.g., lender) entitled to receive claim payments.

**Named Insured**: Person or organization specifically named on policy declarations page with full coverage rights.

**Named Perils**: Policy covering only specifically listed perils (fire, theft, wind, etc.).

**Occurrence**: Accident or continuous/repeated exposure causing injury or damage.

**Occurrence Policy**: Covers incidents occurring during policy period, regardless of when claim is made.

**Per Occurrence Limit**: Maximum amount insurer will pay for single event or claim.

**Policy Period**: Time frame during which policy is active (start date to expiration date).

**Premium**: Payment for insurance coverage, typically annual but may be paid in installments.

**Replacement Cost**: Cost to replace damaged property with new property of similar kind and quality (no depreciation).

**Retention / Self-Insured Retention (SIR)**: Amount insured retains before insurance applies (similar to deductible but insured handles claims).

**Subrogation**: Insurer's right to pursue third parties responsible for loss after paying claim.

**Sublimit**: Maximum amount insurer will pay for specific type of loss (less than overall policy limit).

**Tail Coverage**: Extended reporting period endorsement for claims-made policies, covering claims made after policy expires for incidents during policy period.

**Umbrella Liability**: Excess liability providing broader coverage than underlying policies and filling some gaps.

**Underwriter**: Insurance professional evaluating risk and determining coverage terms, conditions, and pricing.

**Waiver of Subrogation**: Agreement by insurer not to pursue recovery from third party responsible for loss.

### Property Insurance Terms

**Agreed Value**: Insurer and insured agree on property value upfront, eliminating coinsurance penalty risk.

**Business Interruption (BI)**: Coverage for lost income and continuing expenses when business operations suspended due to covered property damage.

**Contingent Business Interruption (CBI)**: Coverage when supplier or customer suffers covered property damage affecting your operations.

**Extra Expense**: Coverage for additional costs incurred to continue operations after property loss (temporary location, expedited shipping).

**Named Storm Deductible**: Percentage deductible (e.g., 2-5% of insured value) applying when loss caused by hurricane or named storm.

**Ordinance or Law Coverage**: Pays increased reconstruction costs due to updated building codes.

**Schedule of Values**: Detailed listing of property locations and values for insurance purposes.

**Spoilage Coverage**: Covers inventory loss when refrigeration or climate control fails.

**Stock Throughput**: Comprehensive cargo coverage from origin through warehousing to final destination.

### Liability Insurance Terms

**Bodily Injury**: Physical harm to person (injury, sickness, disease, death).

**Defense Costs**: Legal fees and expenses to defend claim (may be inside or outside policy limit).

**Duty to Defend**: Insurer's obligation to provide legal defense regardless of claim merit.

**Indemnification**: Agreement to compensate another party for loss, typically through contractual risk transfer.

**Joint and Several Liability**: Multiple parties held responsible; plaintiff can collect full judgment from any one party.

**Personal Injury**: Liability coverage for non-physical harm (defamation, false arrest, invasion of privacy).

**Products-Completed Operations**: Liability for products sold or work completed after relinquishing control.

**Property Damage**: Physical injury to tangible property or loss of use of property.

### Professional Liability Terms

**Errors & Omissions (E&O)**: Insurance for professional service providers against claims of negligent work.

**Prior Acts Date**: Earliest date for which professional liability policy provides retroactive coverage.

**Professional Services**: Activities requiring specialized knowledge, skill, or license forming basis for E&O coverage.

### Workers Compensation Terms

**Experience Modification Rate (EMR / Mod)**: Factor adjusting workers comp premiums based on claim history vs. industry average (1.0 = average; <1.0 = better; >1.0 = worse).

**Lost Time Injury**: Workplace injury resulting in employee missing work beyond day of incident.

**Modified Duty**: Temporary assignment allowing injured employee to work within medical restrictions.

**Return to Work**: Programs and policies facilitating injured employees resuming employment.

**Temporary Total Disability (TTD)**: Benefits paid when employee cannot work during recovery (typically 2/3 of wages).

**Occupational Disease**: Illness arising from workplace exposures (asbestosis, repetitive strain, hearing loss).

### Employee Benefits Terms

**Health Savings Account (HSA)**: Tax-advantaged account paired with high-deductible health plan for medical expenses.

**High-Deductible Health Plan (HDHP)**: Plan with higher deductibles but lower premiums, HSA-eligible.

**Out-of-Pocket Maximum**: Annual limit on member cost-sharing (deductibles, copays, coinsurance); plan pays 100% after.

**Summary Plan Description (SPD)**: Document explaining employee benefit plan terms, required under ERISA.

**Stop-Loss Insurance**: Coverage protecting self-funded health plans against catastrophic claims (specific or aggregate).

### Cyber Insurance Terms

**First-Party Coverage**: Costs to insured organization (forensics, notification, business interruption).

**Third-Party Coverage**: Liability to others (lawsuits, regulatory fines).

**Ransomware Sublimit**: Maximum insurer will pay for ransomware-specific losses (often lower than overall cyber limit).

**Social Engineering**: Coverage for losses from fraudulent instructions appearing to come from trusted sources.

**War & Cyber War Exclusion**: Exclusion for nation-state cyber attacks deemed acts of war.

### Risk Management Terms

**Enterprise Risk Management (ERM)**: Comprehensive approach to identifying, assessing, and managing all organizational risks.

**Key Risk Indicators (KRIs)**: Metrics providing early warning of increasing risk exposure.

**Probable Maximum Loss (PML)**: Estimated maximum property loss from single event (e.g., earthquake, hurricane) with reasonable probability.

**Risk Appetite**: Amount and type of risk organization willing to accept in pursuit of objectives.

**Risk Tolerance**: Acceptable variation around risk appetite; thresholds triggering action.

**Total Cost of Risk (TCOR)**: Sum of insurance premiums, retained losses, risk management expenses, and indirect costs.

## Insurance Planning Checklists

### New Business Insurance Checklist

**Business Information**:
- [ ] Legal entity type (LLC, C-Corp, S-Corp, Partnership, Sole Proprietor)
- [ ] Federal Employer ID Number (FEIN)
- [ ] State business registration/license
- [ ] Business address and additional locations
- [ ] Industry classification (NAICS code)
- [ ] Annual revenue (projected for new businesses)
- [ ] Number of employees and payroll
- [ ] Website URL and business description

**Essential Coverage to Consider**:
- [ ] General Liability ($1M/$2M minimum)
- [ ] Professional Liability if providing services ($1M+)
- [ ] Property Insurance for buildings, equipment, inventory
- [ ] Business Interruption & Extra Expense
- [ ] Workers Compensation (required in most states with employees)
- [ ] Commercial Auto (if vehicles owned or used for business)
- [ ] Cyber Insurance ($100K-$1M for small businesses)
- [ ] Employment Practices Liability (EPLI) with 5+ employees

**Additional Information Needed**:
- [ ] Copy of lease or deed
- [ ] Property valuations or recent appraisals
- [ ] Equipment list and values
- [ ] Vehicle information (VIN, year, make, model)
- [ ] Contracts requiring insurance
- [ ] Loss history (if existing business)

**Timeline**:
- Start 2-4 weeks before coverage needed
- Some carriers offer immediate binding for standard risks

### Annual Insurance Review Checklist

**Business Changes**:
- [ ] Revenue increase/decrease >20%?
- [ ] New locations, closed locations, or relocations?
- [ ] Acquisitions, mergers, or divestitures?
- [ ] New products, services, or business lines?
- [ ] International expansion?
- [ ] Headcount changes (employees, contractors)?
- [ ] New vehicles or equipment purchases?
- [ ] Changes in property values?

**Claims & Risk Profile**:
- [ ] Review past year's claims (frequency, severity, trends)
- [ ] Near-misses or unreported incidents?
- [ ] Changes in operations affecting risk?
- [ ] New regulations or compliance requirements?
- [ ] Contracts requiring insurance updates?

**Coverage Review**:
- [ ] Are policy limits adequate for current exposures?
- [ ] Any coverage gaps or exclusions problematic?
- [ ] Are deductibles appropriate?
- [ ] Certificates of insurance current for all vendors?
- [ ] Named insureds and additional insureds accurate?

**Market Conditions**:
- [ ] How have rates changed in your industry?
- [ ] New coverage options or products available?
- [ ] Alternative risk transfer opportunities (captives, high deductibles)?
- [ ] Any carrier financial strength concerns?

**Action Items**:
- [ ] Update schedule of values (property, equipment, locations)
- [ ] Verify payroll and revenue figures for audit
- [ ] Renewal strategy (incumbent vs. market test)
- [ ] Budget for renewal (anticipate market trends)

### M&A Insurance Checklist

**Pre-LOI (Letter of Intent)**:
- [ ] Engage Marsh M&A practice early
- [ ] Preliminary Rep & Warranty Insurance (RWI) feasibility
- [ ] Target company insurance summary request

**Due Diligence Phase**:
- [ ] Obtain 5-10 years of target's loss runs
- [ ] Review all target's current insurance policies
- [ ] Identify potential tail coverage needs (D&O, E&O, EPLI)
- [ ] Assess unknown/uninsured liabilities
- [ ] Environmental due diligence and pollution liability
- [ ] Product liability history and exposures
- [ ] Workers compensation reserves and open claims
- [ ] RWI underwriting process initiated

**Pre-Close**:
- [ ] Finalize Rep & Warranty Insurance if proceeding
- [ ] Determine tail coverage strategy for claims-made policies
- [ ] Plan for integration into acquirer's insurance program
- [ ] Or continue target's standalone policies temporarily
- [ ] Risk allocation in purchase agreement
- [ ] Notice provisions to target's insurers

**Post-Close**:
- [ ] Integrate target into acquirer's program at renewal
- [ ] Update named insureds and covered entities
- [ ] Consolidate or separate limits
- [ ] Address any claims from transaction process
- [ ] Monitor legacy exposures and tail periods

### Certificate of Insurance Request Guide

**Information to Provide**:
- **Certificate Holder**: Legal name and address of party requiring certificate
- **Project/Contract**: Description and location
- **Coverage Requirements**: Specific types and limits required
  - General Liability: $X per occurrence / $X aggregate
  - Auto Liability: $X combined single limit
  - Workers Compensation: Statutory limits
  - Umbrella: $X per occurrence
  - Professional Liability / Cyber / Other: As specified
- **Additional Insured**: "Certificate Holder as Additional Insured" (if required)
- **Waiver of Subrogation**: Check if required
- **Primary & Non-Contributory**: Check if required
- **Notice of Cancellation**: Days of notice required (30 or 60 typical)
- **Contract Provisions**: Copy of insurance requirements section

**Common Certificate Mistakes to Avoid**:
- Generic certificates without additional insured endorsements (must be on policy)
- Blanket additional insured language not matching contract requirements
- Incorrect policy numbers or limits
- Expired or incorrect effective dates
- Missing waiver of subrogation endorsement

**Timing**: Request certificates 3-5 business days in advance for routine needs; 24 hours available for urgent requests

## Claims Management Guide

### How to Report a Claim

**Immediate Steps** (within 24-48 hours):
1. **Ensure Safety**: Attend to injuries, secure property, prevent further damage
2. **Document**: Photos, videos, witness statements, incident reports
3. **Notify Marsh**: Contact your account team or claims hotline
4. **Preserve Evidence**: Don't repair/dispose until adjuster inspects
5. **Do Not Admit Fault**: Cooperate but reserve liability determinations for professionals

**Information Needed**:
- Policy number and insured's name
- Date, time, and location of loss
- Description of incident and damages
- Injured parties (names, contact info, extent of injuries)
- Witnesses (names and contact information)
- Police/fire department report numbers
- Estimated amount of loss
- Photos and documentation

**Marsh Claims Hotline**: 1-866-MARSH-4U (24/7)

### What to Expect After Reporting

**Within 24-48 Hours**:
- Claim assigned to adjuster
- Adjuster contacts you to schedule inspection/interview
- Initial reserve set by carrier

**First Week**:
- Adjuster inspects property or investigates incident
- Coverage review and determination
- Emergency mitigation (temporary repairs, equipment rental)
- Vendor coordination (restoration contractors, experts)

**Ongoing**:
- Regular updates from adjuster (weekly to monthly depending on complexity)
- Documentation requests (invoices, records, statements)
- Negotiation on scope and amounts
- Settlement discussions

**Timeline to Resolution**:
- Simple property claims: 30-60 days
- Complex property/BI claims: 3-12 months
- Liability claims: 90 days to 2+ years (if litigation)
- Workers comp: Ongoing through medical recovery

### Claim Documentation Tips

**Property Claims**:
- Photos/videos of all damage before repairs
- Detailed inventory of damaged contents (photos, receipts, descriptions)
- Repair estimates from multiple contractors
- Temporary repair invoices (mitigation)
- Business interruption records (lost sales, extra expenses)

**Liability Claims**:
- Incident reports filed immediately
- Witness statements (written and signed)
- Employee training records
- Maintenance logs demonstrating proper care
- Photos of scene and conditions

**Workers Compensation**:
- Accident report completed day of injury
- Medical records and treatment plans
- Modified duty availability
- Payroll records for wage verification
- Return-to-work communications

**Cyber Claims**:
- Forensic investigation reports
- Legal counsel communications (privileged)
- Notification lists and costs
- Business interruption financial data
- Ransom communications (if applicable)

### Claims Advocacy

**Marsh's Role**:
- Liaison between you and insurance carrier
- Ensure proper coverage application
- Negotiate on your behalf
- Expedite claim resolution
- Escalate issues within carrier
- Post-claim analysis and prevention recommendations

**When to Escalate**:
- Coverage denial you disagree with
- Unreasonable settlement offers
- Adjuster unresponsive or unprofessional
- Delays exceeding reasonable timelines
- Complex coverage interpretation issues
