## Property & Business Interruption Claims

### Scenario 1: Manufacturing Facility Fire

**Company Profile**: Mid-size manufacturer, $50M annual revenue, 200 employees

**What Happened**:
A fire started in the electrical equipment room at 2 AM on a Saturday. The sprinkler system activated but the fire caused significant smoke and water damage throughout the 100,000 sq ft facility. Production was halted for 4 months during cleanup and equipment replacement.

**Coverage Analysis**:

**Property Insurance Response**:
- **Building Damage**: $2.5M (smoke damage, structural repairs, electrical systems)
- **Equipment Damage**: $4.2M (manufacturing equipment, computers, office furniture)
- **Inventory Loss**: $1.8M (raw materials and finished goods damaged by smoke/water)
- **Debris Removal**: $350K (hazardous waste disposal, demolition)
- **Total Property Claim**: $8.85M

**Business Interruption Insurance Response**:
- **Lost Profits**: $6.2M (4 months production downtime)
- **Continuing Expenses**: $1.8M (payroll, rent, utilities, loan payments)
- **Extra Expense**: $900K (temporary warehouse, expedited equipment delivery, overtime)
- **Total BI Claim**: $8.9M

**Total Insurance Recovery**: $17.75M  
**Out-of-Pocket Costs** (deductible + uncovered items): $500K

**Claims Timeline**:
- **Day 1 (Saturday 2 AM)**: Fire discovered, fire department called, immediate photos/video taken
- **Day 1 (8 AM)**: Broker notified, emergency mitigation contractors engaged
- **Day 3 (Monday)**: Formal claim filed, adjuster assigned
- **Day 5**: Independent fire investigator and forensic accountant engaged
- **Week 2**: Adjuster site visit, scope of damage assessed
- **Week 4**: Building engineer and equipment appraisers complete reports
- **Week 6**: Partial advance payment ($2M) received for immediate repairs
- **Month 3**: Interim BI payments begin ($500K/month)
- **Month 6**: Production resumes, final property payment received
- **Month 9**: Final BI accounting completed, claim closed
- **Total Claims Duration**: 9 months

**Lessons Learned**:

✅ **What Went Right**:
- **Emergency Response Plan**: Documented emergency contacts, immediate broker notification
- **Documentation**: Pre-loss equipment inventory with photos and serial numbers
- **Business Continuity**: Backup suppliers enabled partial production at alternate site
- **Financial Records**: Clean, organized accounting enabled fast BI claim
- **Insurance Adequacy**: Policy limits were sufficient (often not the case)

❌ **What Could Have Been Better**:
- **Fire Suppression**: Older electrical room lacked modern fire suppression (just sprinklers)
- **Hot Work Permit**: Contractor had done welding near electrical room day before (potential cause)
- **Equipment Breakdown Coverage**: Some losses were equipment failure-related, not pure fire
- **Supply Chain BI**: Customer losses from delayed deliveries not covered (could have added contingent BI)

**Cost Breakdown**:
```
Property Damage:          $8,850,000
Business Interruption:    $8,900,000
Deductible:                ($250,000)
Uninsured Items:           ($250,000)
───────────────────────────────────
Net Insurance Recovery:   $17,250,000
```

**Prevention Checklist**:
- ☐ Annual infrared electrical scans
- ☐ Modern fire suppression in equipment rooms (FM-200, Novec 1230)
- ☐ Hot work permit program with 24-hour fire watch
- ☐ Regular sprinkler system inspections
- ☐ Equipment maintenance program with thermal imaging
- ☐ Business continuity plan with alternate production sites
- ☐ Pre-loss equipment inventory with values and serial numbers

### Scenario 2: Warehouse Roof Collapse (Snow Load)

**Company Profile**: Distribution company, 250,000 sq ft warehouse, $30M inventory

**What Happened**:
After a severe winter storm with 36" of snow accumulation, a section of the warehouse roof collapsed under the weight. Snow and debris damaged $8M in inventory. Business operations were disrupted for 2 months.

**Coverage Analysis**:

**Property Insurance Response**:
- **Building Damage**: $1.2M (roof structure, HVAC, lighting)
- **Inventory Damage**: $8.3M (crushed and water-damaged goods)
- **Cleanup/Debris**: $180K
- **Total Property Claim**: $9.68M

**Business Interruption**: Not applicable (no coverage due to policy exclusion)

**Key Issue - Coverage Gap**: The policy excluded BI coverage for delays caused by lack of materials/stock. Since the warehouse wasn't the production facility (just storage), BI didn't trigger. **Lesson: Stock throughput/contingent BI coverage was needed.**

**Claims Timeline**:
- **Day 1**: Roof collapse discovered Monday morning
- **Day 1**: Emergency tarping and stabilization ($45K emergency expense)
- **Day 2**: Claim filed, emergency advance payment requested
- **Week 1**: Structural engineer assessment, inventory salvage begins
- **Week 2**: Advance payment received ($2M) for immediate repairs
- **Month 2**: Roof reconstruction begins
- **Month 4**: Repairs complete, inventory restocked
- **Month 6**: Final claim settlement

**Total Insurance Recovery**: $9.68M  
**Out-of-Pocket** (deductible + lost business): $100K + lost profits ~$3M (uninsured)

**Lessons Learned**:

❌ **Major Gap**:
- **No Contingent BI/Extra Expense**: Business income loss from lack of inventory wasn't covered
- **Inadequate Roof Maintenance**: Roof was 20+ years old, overdue for reinforcement
- **No Snow Load Monitoring**: Should have had emergency snow removal protocol

✅ **What Helped**:
- **Inventory Records**: Detailed SKU-level inventory made claim straightforward
- **Alternate Storage**: Some inventory at offsite facility maintained partial operations
- **Customer Communication**: Transparent communication retained customer relationships

**Prevention Checklist**:
- ☐ Roof structural inspections every 5 years
- ☐ Snow load monitoring and removal plan for buildings in snow-prone areas
- ☐ Stock throughput or contingent BI coverage for warehouses
- ☐ Distributed inventory across multiple locations
- ☐ Building code compliance (many older buildings don't meet current snow load requirements)

## Cyber & Data Breach Claims

### Scenario 3: Ransomware Attack on Healthcare Provider

**Company Profile**: Regional hospital system, 3 facilities, 1,200 employees, 50K patient records

**What Happened**:
At 6 PM on a Friday, IT staff discovered ransomware had encrypted critical systems including EHR (Electronic Health Records), billing, and administrative systems. The attackers demanded $2.5M in Bitcoin. Operations were severely impacted for 3 weeks.

**Coverage Analysis**:

**Cyber Insurance Response**:
- **Forensic Investigation**: $180K (incident response, forensics, root cause analysis)
- **Ransomware Payment**: $0 (organization chose not to pay after consulting with FBI)
- **Data Restoration**: $850K (rebuild systems from backups, data recovery specialists)
- **Business Interruption**: $2.1M (lost revenue from delayed procedures, diverted ambulances)
- **Notification & Credit Monitoring**: $420K (15,000 patients whose data was exfiltrated)
- **Legal & Regulatory**: $680K (attorneys for OCR investigation, state AG inquiry)
- **Public Relations**: $95K (crisis communications, media management)
- **Extortion Consulting**: $40K (ransomware negotiation experts on standby)
- **Total Cyber Claim**: $4.365M

**Healthcare Professional Liability**: $0 (no patient harm - fortunate timing)

**Total Insurance Recovery**: $4.365M  
**Sublimit Note**: Cyber policy had $5M limit with $1M ransomware sublimit (20% coinsurance)

**Claims Timeline**:
- **Friday 6 PM**: Ransomware discovered
- **Friday 7 PM**: Cyber insurer's hotline called, incident response (IR) firm engaged
- **Friday 9 PM**: Systems isolated, IR firm on-site remotely
- **Saturday**: IR team analyzes attack, determines PHI was exfiltrated before encryption
- **Sunday**: Legal counsel engaged, breach notification obligations assessed
- **Week 1**: FBI notified, OCR breach reported (as required within 72 hours for 500+ records)
- **Week 2**: Negotiation with attackers (ultimately declined to pay)
- **Week 3**: System rebuild from backups begins
- **Week 4**: Core clinical systems restored, limited operations resume
- **Week 6**: Full operations restored
- **Month 3**: Breach notification complete (letters, credit monitoring setup)
- **Month 6**: OCR investigation concludes with $750K fine (not covered by insurance)
- **Month 12**: Final claim settlement

**Total Claims Duration**: 12 months

**Lessons Learned**:

❌ **Security Failures**:
- **No MFA**: Single-factor authentication on VPN allowed initial access
- **Unpatched Systems**: Exploited vulnerability had patch available for 4 months
- **Insufficient Backups**: Backups were on same network and also encrypted
- **No EDR**: Endpoint detection would have caught lateral movement
- **Weak Segmentation**: Attackers moved from admin network to clinical systems easily

✅ **What Helped**:
- **Cyber Insurance**: Immediate access to IR firm and legal counsel was invaluable
- **Incident Response Plan**: Documented contacts and decision tree saved time
- **Executive Decision-Making**: Leadership quickly decided not to pay, focused on recovery
- **Communication Plan**: Transparent updates to patients, staff, and regulators maintained trust

**Financial Impact**:
```
Cyber Insurance Recovery:    $4,365,000
OCR Regulatory Fine:           ($750,000) [NOT covered]
Lost Business (uninsured):   ($1,200,000)
Legal Defense (excess):        ($180,000)
Deductible:                     ($50,000)
Reputation Damage:          [Unquantified]
───────────────────────────────────────────
Net Out-of-Pocket:          ($2,180,000)
```

**Prevention Checklist**:
- ☐ Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) on all remote access and privileged accounts
- ☐ Endpoint Detection & Response (EDR) on all devices
- ☐ Offline, immutable backups tested quarterly
- ☐ Patch management with 30-day SLA for critical vulnerabilities
- ☐ Network segmentation between admin, clinical, and guest networks
- ☐ Security awareness training with phishing simulations quarterly
- ☐ Incident response plan tested annually with tabletop exercises
- ☐ Cyber insurance with adequate BI limits and low ransomware coinsurance

### Scenario 4: Business Email Compromise (BEC) - Wire Fraud

**Company Profile**: Private equity firm, $500M AUM, 45 employees

**What Happened**:
The CFO received an email that appeared to be from the CEO (currently traveling overseas) requesting an urgent wire transfer of $2.8M for a "time-sensitive acquisition". The email was very convincing - correct signature block, similar writing style, sense of urgency. The CFO initiated the wire transfer. Two days later, the real CEO returned and revealed the email was fraudulent. Funds had already been transferred through multiple international accounts.

**Coverage Analysis**:

**Crime/Cyber Insurance Response**:
- **Social Engineering Coverage**: $2.5M (policy limit - sublimit for social engineering)
- **Forensic Investigation**: $75K (email forensics, tracing funds)
- **Legal Fees**: $150K (attorneys attempting to recover funds, regulatory defense)
- **Total Claim**: $2.725M

**Recovery Efforts**: $320K eventually recovered through international cooperation (lucky)

**Out-of-Pocket Loss**: $2.48M (difference between $2.8M loss and $2.725M insurance + $320K recovered)

**Claims Timeline**:
- **Day 1 (Monday)**: Fraudulent wire transfer executed
- **Day 3 (Wednesday)**: Fraud discovered when CEO returns
- **Day 3 (3 PM)**: Bank notified, wire recall attempted (too late)
- **Day 3 (5 PM)**: FBI contacted, crime/cyber insurance claim filed
- **Day 4**: Forensic analysis confirms email account compromise
- **Week 1**: Legal counsel engaged to pursue fund recovery
- **Week 2**: Insurance company approves claim under social engineering coverage
- **Month 1**: Advance payment received ($1.5M)
- **Month 6**: Final settlement after recovery efforts exhaust
- **Month 9**: Small recovery ($320K) from frozen overseas account

**Lessons Learned**:

❌ **Control Failures**:
- **No Dual Authorization**: Single person could initiate large wire transfers
- **Email Security**: No email authentication (DMARC, DKIM, SPF)
- **No Verification Protocol**: No requirement to confirm large wires via phone call
- **Spoofed Email**: Attacker registered look-alike domain (rnarsh.com vs marsh.com)

✅ **Good Practices**:
- **Quick Response**: Immediate bank notification gave slim chance of recovery
- **Insurance Coverage**: Had social engineering coverage (many don't)
- **Law Enforcement**: FBI involvement led to eventual partial recovery

**Prevention Checklist**:
- ☐ Dual authorization for wire transfers over threshold (e.g., $50K)
- ☐ Verbal verification required for all wire transfer changes (phone call to known number)
- ☐ Email authentication protocols (DMARC, DKIM, SPF)
- ☐ Email security training specifically on BEC/wire fraud tactics
- ☐ Look-alike domain monitoring and blocking
- ☐ Crime/cyber insurance with adequate social engineering sublimit
- ☐ Regular phishing tests focused on executive impersonation
- ☐ Payment confirmation protocols documented and enforced

**Financial Impact**:
```
Wire Fraud Loss:              $2,800,000
Crime Insurance:             ($2,500,000)
Investigation/Legal:           ($225,000)
Insurance:                      $225,000
Recovered Funds:               $320,000
───────────────────────────────────────
Net Loss:                      $320,000
```

## General Liability & Umbrella Claims

### Scenario 5: Premises Liability - Slip and Fall with Severe Injury

**Company Profile**: Retail store, high-traffic shopping center location

**What Happened**:
A customer slipped on a wet floor near the store entrance during a rainstorm. She fell and fractured her hip, requiring surgery and 6 months of physical therapy. The customer, age 68, had permanent mobility limitations and sued for medical expenses, lost wages, pain and suffering, and future care needs.

**Coverage Analysis**:

**General Liability Insurance Response**:
- **Medical Expenses**: $185K (surgery, hospital stay, rehab, ongoing PT)
- **Lost Wages**: $45K (customer was still working part-time)
- **Pain & Suffering**: $850K (negotiated settlement)
- **Future Medical Care**: $120K (projected ongoing treatment)
- **Legal Defense**: $95K (attorneys, expert witnesses, depositions)
- **Total Claim**: $1.295M

**Settlement**: $1.2M (within GL policy limit of $2M per occurrence)

**Claims Timeline**:
- **Day of Incident**: Store manager documented scene, incident report filed, photos taken
- **Day 2**: Claim reported to insurance carrier, adjuster assigned
- **Week 2**: Adjuster interviews store staff, reviews surveillance video
- **Month 1**: Customer hires attorney, formal demand letter sent
- **Month 3**: Discovery phase begins (depositions, document requests)
- **Month 9**: Mediation unsuccessful (customer demanded $2.5M)
- **Month 12**: Trial date set
- **Month 14**: Settlement reached week before trial ($1.2M)

**Total Claims Duration**: 14 months

**Lessons Learned**:

❌ **Liability Factors**:
- **Delayed Response**: Floor wet for 20+ minutes before accident (per testimony)
- **No Warning Signs**: Wet floor sign not deployed despite rainstorm
- **Inadequate Mopping Protocol**: Entrance should have had mat and continuous monitoring during rain
- **Poor Documentation**: Manager's incident report vague, didn't measure how much water on floor

✅ **What Helped Settlement**:
- **Surveillance Video**: Showed customer not paying attention (comparative negligence)
- **Immediate Care**: Store staff called ambulance, showed concern
- **Insurance Limits**: Adequate limits avoided personal liability for business owner
- **Settlement Negotiation**: Trial would have been expensive and outcome uncertain

**Financial Impact**:
```
Settlement Amount:         $1,200,000
Legal Defense:                $95,000
Total Claim:               $1,295,000
Insurance Coverage:       ($1,295,000)
Deductible:                   $25,000
───────────────────────────────────
Net Out-of-Pocket:            $25,000
```

**Premium Impact**: GL premium increased 35% at renewal after this claim

**Prevention Checklist**:
- ☐ Weather protocols: Deploy mats, wet floor signs, extra mopping during rain/snow
- ☐ Document floor inspections every hour (sign-off log)
- ☐ Staff training on slip/fall prevention and incident response
- ☐ Surveillance cameras covering all high-traffic areas
- ☐ Detailed incident report template (measurements, photos, witness statements)
- ☐ Adequate GL and umbrella insurance limits
- ☐ Annual safety audits of premises
- ☐ Non-slip flooring in entrance areas

## Workers Compensation Claims

### Scenario 6: Workplace Injury with Permanent Disability

**Company Profile**: Construction contractor, 85 employees, commercial projects

**What Happened**:
A carpenter fell 15 feet from scaffolding when a plank gave way. He suffered multiple fractures, head trauma, and spinal cord injury resulting in partial paralysis. He was unable to return to construction work.

**Coverage Analysis**:

**Workers Compensation Insurance Response**:
- **Medical Expenses**: $890K (surgeries, hospital stay, rehab, ongoing care)
- **Temporary Total Disability**: $65K (wage replacement during recovery)
- **Permanent Partial Disability**: $450K (structured payments over lifetime)
- **Vocational Rehabilitation**: $35K (retraining for sedentary work)
- **Legal/Administrative**: $40K (workers comp attorney, hearings)
- **Total WC Claim**: $1.48M

**Reserve Set**: $1.8M (insurance company's initial reserve estimate)

**Additional Exposure**:
- **OSHA Citation**: $75K fine for scaffolding violations (not covered by insurance)
- **Premium Impact**: Experience Mod increased from 0.92 to 1.35 (+47% premium impact for 3 years)

**Claims Timeline**:
- **Day of Accident**: Emergency response, hospitalization, employer notified
- **Day 1**: Workers comp claim filed
- **Week 1**: Investigation by insurer and OSHA
- **Month 1**: OSHA citations issued
- **Month 3**: Employee released from hospital to outpatient rehab
- **Month 6**: Determination of permanent partial disability
- **Month 9**: Vocational rehab assessment completed
- **Year 1**: Settlement of permanent disability payments (structured)
- **Year 2**: Claim remains open for ongoing medical care

**Total Claims Duration**: Ongoing (lifetime medical for catastrophic injury)

**Lessons Learned**:

❌ **Safety Violations**:
- **Scaffolding Not Inspected**: OSHA requires daily inspection by competent person
- **Missing Guardrails**: Scaffolding lacked required fall protection
- **No Fall Arrest System**: Employee not wearing harness despite company policy
- **Untrained Worker**: Employee had not completed fall protection training
- **Inadequate Supervision**: Foreman not on-site to enforce safety

✅ **What Helped**:
- **Immediate Medical Care**: Quick response likely prevented more severe outcome
- **Workers Comp Coverage**: Protected both employee and employer from lawsuit
- **Return-to-Work Program**: Vocational rehab helped employee find new career path

**Financial Impact**:
```
Workers Comp Claim:        $1,480,000
OSHA Fine:                    $75,000
Increased Premiums (3 yrs):  $180,000
───────────────────────────────────────
Total Cost (insurance):    $1,480,000
Total Cost (out-of-pocket):  $255,000
```

**Prevention Checklist**:
- ☐ Daily scaffolding inspection by competent person (documented)
- ☐ Fall protection training for all employees working at heights
- ☐ Enforcement of fall arrest equipment (harness, lanyard, anchor point)
- ☐ Adequate supervision and safety oversight
- ☐ Safety culture with disciplinary procedures for violations
- ☐ Pre-task planning for high-risk activities
- ☐ Regular safety meetings and toolbox talks
- ☐ Incident investigation protocols to identify root causes

## Product Liability Claims

### Scenario 7: Product Defect Causing Injury and Recall

**Company Profile**: Consumer products manufacturer, kitchen appliances, $80M annual revenue

**What Happened**:
A coffee maker sold by the company had a defect that caused overheating, resulting in fires in 47 homes. Three people suffered burns, and one house had extensive fire damage. The company issued a voluntary recall of 250,000 units.

**Coverage Analysis**:

**Product Liability Insurance Response**:
- **Bodily Injury Claims**: $2.8M (medical expenses, pain & suffering for 3 injured consumers)
- **Property Damage Claims**: $950K (fire damage to 47 homes)
- **Product Recall Costs**: $6.2M (notification, return shipping, refunds, disposal)
- **Legal Defense**: $580K (attorneys, expert witnesses, regulatory defense)
- **Crisis Management**: $120K (PR, customer hotline, recall coordination)
- **Regulatory/CPSC Compliance**: $95K (Consumer Product Safety Commission cooperation)
- **Total Product Liability Claim**: $10.745M

**Policy Limits**: $10M per occurrence, $20M aggregate (claim within limits but close)

**Business Impact** (Not Insured):
- **Lost Sales**: $15M (12-month impact on all product lines due to reputation)
- **Retailer Penalties**: $2M (chargebacks from major retailers)
- **Stock Price**: Dropped 28% on recall announcement

**Claims Timeline**:
- **Month 1**: First fire reported, product investigation begins
- **Month 2**: Second and third fires, pattern identified
- **Month 3**: Company notifies CPSC, voluntary recall announced
- **Month 3**: Product liability claim filed, crisis management team engaged
- **Month 4**: Recall website launched, consumer hotline operational
- **Month 6**: Injured consumers file lawsuits
- **Month 9**: Property damage claims from homeowners
- **Year 1**: Mediation with injured parties
- **Year 2**: All injury claims settled
- **Year 3**: Final recall costs tallied, claim closed

**Total Claims Duration**: 3 years

**Lessons Learned**:

❌ **Product Failures**:
- **Design Flaw**: Thermal fuse was undersized for high-wattage heating element
- **Testing Inadequate**: Accelerated life testing didn't catch the overheating failure mode
- **Supplier Change**: Fuse supplier changed without adequate validation
- **No Field Monitoring**: Company had no system to track field failures and identify patterns

✅ **Good Crisis Response**:
- **Quick Recall**: Voluntary recall before CPSC mandated it (showed good faith)
- **Consumer Communication**: Clear, empathetic messaging and easy return process
- **Generous Remediation**: Full refunds plus $50 gift card (not required but good PR)
- **Insurance Coverage**: Product recall coverage was critical (often excluded)

**Financial Impact**:
```
Product Liability Insurance:  $10,745,000
Lost Sales/Revenue:           ($15,000,000)
Retailer Chargebacks:          ($2,000,000)
Deductible:                      ($100,000)
Premium Increase (3 yrs):      ($1,200,000)
────────────────────────────────────────────
Total Cost (insurance):        $10,745,000
Total Cost (out-of-pocket):   ($18,300,000)
```

**Prevention Checklist**:
- ☐ Rigorous design validation and testing (FMEA, accelerated life testing)
- ☐ Supplier qualification and change control procedures
- ☐ Field failure monitoring and early warning system
- ☐ Product liability insurance with adequate limits ($10M+ for consumer products)
- ☐ Product recall expense coverage (often separate or sublimit)
- ☐ Crisis management plan with pre-identified recall logistics partner
- ☐ Quality management system (ISO 9001) with continuous improvement
- ☐ Safety certifications (UL, ETL) with annual audits

**Company Profile**: Public software company, $300M market cap, recent IPO (18 months ago)

**What Happened**:
The company announced a revenue restatement, reducing previously reported Q2 and Q3 revenue by 15%. The stock price dropped 42% on the announcement. **Coverage Analysis**:

**D&O Insurance Response** (3-layer tower):

- **Layer 2 (Excess)**: $10M limit

- **Legal Defense**: $4.8M (securities law firm, forensic accounting, expert witnesses)

- Company paid $2M retention

**Individual Impact**:
- **CEO**: Named defendant, underwent depositions, significant personal stress
- **CFO**: Resigned under pressure, difficulty finding new role

- **Month 1**: D&O claim filed, defense counsel selected

- **Year 3.5**: SEC investigation closes with no charges (good outcome)

**Total Claims Duration**: 3.5 years

**Lessons Learned**:

❌ **Governance Failures**:
- **Revenue Recognition Issues**: Improper timing of revenue recognition (ASC 606 compliance failure)
- **Internal Controls**: Weak financial reporting controls identified
- **Disclosure Controls**: Inadequate review of public disclosures

✅ **What Limited Damages**:
- **Self-Disclosure**: Company voluntarily restated and disclosed (rather than waiting for auditor)
- **Cooperation**: Full cooperation with SEC investigation
- **Remediation**: Hired new CFO, strengthened internal controls
- **D&O Insurance**: Adequate tower prevented personal liability for directors/officers

**Financial Impact**:
```
Settlement:                   $18,000,000
```

- ☐ Strong internal controls over financial reporting (SOX 404 compliance)

- ☐ Engaged audit committee with financial expertise
- ☐ Regular D&O insurance reviews (IPOs need higher limits)
- ☐ Directors & Officers training on fiduciary duties and securities law
- ☐ Legal review of earnings calls and investor presentations

## Key Takeaways Across All Scenarios

### Common Themes in Successful Claims Management

1. **Documentation**: Pre-loss documentation (inventory, maintenance records, policies) is critical
2. **Immediate Response**: Quick notification to insurer and engagement of experts
3. **Broker Partnership**: Marsh brokers facilitated claims process and advocacy
4. **Insurance Adequacy**: Sufficient limits prevented out-of-pocket exposure
5. **Risk Management**: Best claims outcomes came from companies with strong risk management

### Common Mistakes Leading to Uninsured Losses

1. **Inadequate Limits**: Many claims exceeded available insurance (plan for worst case)
2. **Coverage Gaps**: Missing endorsements (contingent BI, social engineering, recall expense)
3. **Policy Exclusions**: Assumption that "everything is covered" - read your policy
4. **Delayed Reporting**: Waiting to report claims can prejudice coverage
5. **Poor Documentation**: Inability to prove value of loss or extent of damages

### Claims Best Practices

**Before a Loss**:
- ☐ Annual insurance reviews with broker to ensure adequate limits
- ☐ Document property values, equipment, inventory
- ☐ Implement risk management programs (safety, cybersecurity, quality)
- ☐ Create emergency response plans with insurance contact information
- ☐ Review policy exclusions and consider endorsements to fill gaps

**During a Loss**:
- ☐ Ensure safety first (life safety > property preservation)
- ☐ Document the scene (photos, videos, measurements, witness statements)
- ☐ Notify insurer and broker immediately (within 24 hours)
- ☐ Engage experts early (forensic accountants, engineers, legal counsel)
- ☐ Mitigate further damage (duty to mitigate)
- ☐ Preserve evidence and maintain chain of custody

**After a Loss**:
- ☐ Conduct root cause analysis to prevent recurrence
- ☐ Update insurance coverage based on lessons learned
- ☐ Implement corrective actions identified during investigation
- ☐ Review emergency response plan performance and update
- ☐ Share lessons learned with employees and leadership

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How quickly should I report a claim?**

A: **Immediately - within 24 hours if possible.** Most policies require "prompt" notification. Delays can prejudice coverage, especially for liability claims where early investigation is critical.

**Q: Should I report every incident, even if I'm not sure it's a claim?**

A: **Yes, report anything that could potentially become a claim.** You can always close it if it doesn't develop. It's better to over-report than risk late reporting issues.

**Q: Will reporting a claim always increase my premiums?**

A: **Not necessarily.** Frequency matters more than individual claims. One large claim from an unpreventable event may have less impact than multiple small preventable claims. Your loss history is evaluated over 3-5 years.

**Q: Can I choose my own adjuster or attorney?**

A: **For defense counsel (liability claims), you usually can select from insurer's panel.** For property appraisers, you can hire your own public adjuster, but they take a fee (typically 10% of recovery).

**Q: What is a claims reserve, and why does it matter?**

A: **An insurer's estimate of what a claim will ultimately cost.** High reserves can impact your loss history and premiums. You can work with your broker to ensure reserves are reasonable and reduced as claims close favorably.

**Q: What happens if my claim exceeds my policy limits?**

A: **You pay the excess out-of-pocket (or from umbrella/excess policies).** This is why adequate limits are critical. Marsh can model worst-case scenarios to ensure sufficient coverage.

**Q: How long do claims typically take to resolve?**

A: **Varies widely:**
- Property: 6-12 months (simple) to 2+ years (complex/litigated)
- Cyber: 6-18 months
- Liability: 1-5 years (if litigated)
- Workers Comp: Ongoing for serious injuries
- D&O: 2-5 years for securities litigation

**Q: What documentation should I keep for potential claims?**

A: **Key documents:**
- Property: Appraisals, photos, purchase receipts, maintenance records
- Inventory: Detailed listings with values and photos
- Financial: P&Ls, balance sheets, payroll records, tax returns
- Safety: Training records, inspection reports, incident logs
- Contracts: Agreements with customers, vendors, subcontractors
- IT: Network diagrams, security logs, backups

**Q: Can I settle a claim directly with the claimant?**

A: **No - always involve your insurer first.** Settling without insurer consent can void coverage. Insurers have duty to defend and control settlement negotiations.
