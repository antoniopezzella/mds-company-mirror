## Common Cyber Risks Businesses Face

Businesses today face numerous cyber threats that can significantly impact operations and finances:

### Data Breaches
Unauthorized access to sensitive customer, employee, or business data. Data breaches can result in regulatory fines, legal liabilities, and reputational damage. Common causes include phishing attacks, weak passwords, and unpatched software vulnerabilities.

### Ransomware Attacks
Malicious software that encrypts business data and demands payment for its release. Ransomware can halt operations, cause significant downtime, and lead to permanent data loss. Modern ransomware groups often threaten to publish stolen data if ransom isn't paid.

### Business Email Compromise (BEC)
Sophisticated phishing attacks targeting employees to fraudulently transfer funds or share sensitive information. BEC attacks cost businesses billions annually and are increasingly difficult to detect.

### Supply Chain Attacks
Cyber attacks targeting vendors, suppliers, or third-party service providers to gain access to larger organizations. These attacks exploit trust relationships in the business ecosystem.

### DDoS Attacks
Distributed Denial of Service attacks overwhelm systems with traffic, making websites and services unavailable to legitimate users. This causes business interruption and potential revenue loss.

### Insider Threats
Security risks from employees, contractors, or business partners who have authorized access to systems. May be malicious or accidental, but can cause significant damage. Include data theft, sabotage, and unintentional policy violations.

### Social Engineering
Manipulation tactics used to trick employees into revealing sensitive information or granting system access. Includes pretexting, baiting, and vishing (voice phishing) attacks.

### IoT and Connected Device Vulnerabilities
Security weaknesses in Internet of Things devices, industrial control systems, and smart building technology that can serve as entry points for attackers.

### Cloud Security Risks
Misconfigured cloud storage, inadequate access controls, and data residency concerns in cloud computing environments.

## Cyber Risk Prevention Strategies

Marsh helps organizations implement comprehensive cybersecurity programs:

### Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
Requiring multiple forms of verification reduces unauthorized access risk by over 99% compared to passwords alone.

### Security Awareness Training
Regular employee training on phishing recognition, password hygiene, and security best practices significantly reduces human error incidents.

### Patch Management
Systematic process for identifying, testing, and deploying security updates to address known vulnerabilities.

### Data Encryption
Encrypting sensitive data at rest and in transit protects information even if systems are compromised.

### Network Segmentation
Dividing networks into isolated zones limits lateral movement if attackers breach perimeter defenses.

### Offsite Backups
Maintaining secure, tested backups in separate locations ensures recovery capability after ransomware or system failures.

### Incident Response Planning
Pre-established procedures, vendor relationships, and communication protocols enable rapid containment and recovery from cyber events.

## Regulatory & Compliance Landscape

### United States Federal Regulations

**SEC Cybersecurity Disclosure Rules (2023)**:
- Public companies must disclose material cyber incidents within 4 business days
- Annual reporting on cybersecurity risk management, strategy, and governance
- Board oversight and management expertise disclosure required

**FTC Data Security Requirements**:
- Reasonable security measures to protect consumer data
- Enforcement actions for inadequate security (e.g., Equifax $575M settlement)
- Privacy rules under FTC Act Section 5

**HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act)**:
- Applies to healthcare providers, insurers, and business associates
- Security Rule requires administrative, physical, and technical safeguards
- Breach notification requirements for protected health information (PHI)
- Penalties: $100-$50,000 per violation, up to $1.5M annually per violation type

**GLBA (Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act)**:
- Financial institutions must protect customer financial information
- Safeguards Rule requires risk assessments, employee training, vendor oversight
- FTC and banking regulators enforce

**FISMA (Federal Information Security Management Act)**:
- Applies to federal agencies and contractors handling government data
- NIST 800-53 controls required
- Regular security assessments and continuous monitoring

### State Privacy & Security Laws

**CCPA/CPRA (California)**:
- California Consumer Privacy Act and Consumer Privacy Rights Act
- Applies to businesses with CA residents' data meeting revenue/volume thresholds
- Consumer rights: Access, deletion, opt-out of sale, correct inaccurate data
- Private right of action for data breaches ($100-$750 per consumer)
- Enforcement by California Privacy Protection Agency

**New York SHIELD Act**:
- Requires reasonable data security safeguards
- Breach notification within "without unreasonable delay"
- Applies to any business with NY resident data

**State Breach Notification Laws**:
- All 50 states have breach notification requirements
- Timelines vary: Immediate to 90 days, most require "without unreasonable delay"
- Attorney General notification often required
- Consumer notification requirements and methods specified

**Colorado Privacy Act, Virginia CDPA, Connecticut Data Privacy Act**:
- Comprehensive privacy laws similar to CCPA
- Consumer rights to access, delete, opt-out
- Data protection assessments for high-risk processing

### International Regulations

**GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation - EU)**:
- Applies to EU residents' data regardless of company location
- Lawful basis required for processing (consent, contract, legitimate interest)
- Data subject rights: Access, erasure, portability, restriction
- Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) for high-risk processing
- Breach notification within 72 hours to supervisory authority
- Fines: Up to €20M or 4% of global revenue (whichever is higher)
- Extra-territorial reach affects US companies serving EU customers

**UK GDPR & Data Protection Act 2018**:
- Post-Brexit UK version of GDPR with similar requirements
- ICO (Information Commissioner's Office) enforcement
- International data transfer requirements

**PIPEDA (Canada)**:
- Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act
- Consent, accountability, transparency principles
- Provincial laws in Quebec (Bill 64), Alberta, BC

**LGPD (Brazil)**:
- Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados
- GDPR-inspired comprehensive privacy law
- ANPD (National Data Protection Authority) enforcement
- Similar fines and breach notification requirements

**China - Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL)**:
- Comprehensive data privacy framework
- Strict data localization and cross-border transfer rules
- Separate consent for sensitive data
- Cybersecurity reviews for critical information infrastructure

**Australia - Privacy Act & Notifiable Data Breaches**:
- APP (Australian Privacy Principles) for handling personal information
- Mandatory breach notification for eligible data breaches
- OAIC (Office of the Australian Information Commissioner) enforcement

### Industry-Specific Requirements

**PCI-DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard)**:
- Applies to any organization processing credit/debit card transactions
- 12 security requirements covering network security, access control, monitoring
- Annual assessments and quarterly vulnerability scans
- Violations can result in fines from card brands and loss of processing privileges

**NYDFS Cybersecurity Regulation (23 NYCRR 500)**:
- Applies to financial services companies operating in New York
- CISO designation, annual risk assessments, incident response plans
- Multi-factor authentication, encryption, audit trails
- Annual compliance certifications required

**NERC CIP (Critical Infrastructure Protection)**:
- Applies to electric utilities and bulk power system operators
- Physical and cyber security standards for critical assets
- Regular audits and substantial penalties for non-compliance

**CMMC (Cybersecurity Maturity Model Certification)**:
- Required for DoD contractors handling Federal Contract Information (FCI) or Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI)
- Tiered maturity levels (1-3) based on data sensitivity
- Third-party assessments required for higher levels
