## Overview

Technology companies face unique and rapidly evolving risks that traditional insurance policies weren't designed to cover. From software failures to data breaches, IP disputes to securities litigation, tech firms require specialized coverage tailored to digital business models.

### Why Tech Insurance is Different

**Intangible Assets**:
- Most value is in IP, data, and software (not physical assets)
- Traditional property insurance doesn't cover digital assets
- Breach of customer data can be catastrophic

**Rapid Growth & Change**:
- Scaling from startup to unicorn creates new exposures
- Frequent pivots and product launches
- M&A activity (acquisitions create new liabilities)
- Geographic expansion (international compliance)

**High-Stakes Liability**:
- Single software bug can affect millions of users
- Data breaches expose sensitive customer information
- SLA failures trigger contractual penalties
- Securities litigation common for high-growth companies

**Regulatory Complexity**:
- GDPR, CCPA, and global privacy laws
- SEC regulations for publicly traded companies
- Industry-specific compliance (HIPAA for health tech, PCI-DSS for payments)
- AI/ML regulations emerging globally

### Essential Coverage for Tech Companies

| Coverage Type | Who Needs It | Typical Limits | Annual Cost |
|---------------|--------------|----------------|-------------|
| **Tech E&O** | All tech companies | $1M-$10M | $5K-$100K |
| **Cyber Liability** | All (especially SaaS) | $5M-$50M | $15K-$300K |
| **D&O Liability** | Funded startups, public cos | $5M-$100M+ | $15K-$500K+ |
| **General Liability** | All with offices/events | $1M-$5M | $2K-$15K |
| **Property** | Hardware companies | Actual value + BI | $3K-$30K |
| **Workers Comp** | All with employees | Statutory + $1M EL | $5K-$100K+ |
| **EPLI** | All with employees (5+) | $1M-$10M | $3K-$50K |
| **Crime/Fidelity** | All companies | $1M-$10M | $2K-$15K |
| **IP Defense** | Patent-heavy companies | $1M-$5M | $10K-$50K |

## Technology Errors & Omissions (E&O)

### Coverage Overview

Tech E&O (also called Professional Liability or Technology Liability) protects against claims arising from **professional services, software defects, and technology failures**.

**What's Covered**:
- ✅ **Software defects and bugs**: Code errors causing customer losses
- ✅ **Failure to deliver**: Late or incomplete project delivery
- ✅ **Service interruptions**: Downtime or performance issues
- ✅ **Data loss**: Accidental deletion or corruption of customer data
- ✅ **SLA breaches**: Failure to meet uptime or performance guarantees
- ✅ **Negligent advice**: Poor consulting or implementation guidance
- ✅ **Misrepresentation**: Product claims that don't match actual capabilities
- ✅ **Integration failures**: Software doesn't work with third-party systems
- ✅ **Defense costs**: Legal fees and expert witnesses

**What's NOT Covered**:
- ❌ **Intentional acts**: Fraud, criminal behavior
- ❌ **Known issues**: Problems you knew about before policy inception
- ❌ **Bodily injury/property damage**: Use General Liability
- ❌ **Data breach**: Use Cyber Liability (though some E&O includes it)
- ❌ **Contractual guarantees**: Warranties and performance bonds
- ❌ **IP infringement by others**: Use IP Abatement/Defense coverage

### Common E&O Claims in Tech

**Software-as-a-Service (SaaS)**:
- Platform outage causes customer business interruption ($2M-$10M claims)
- Data corruption or loss from software bug
- Security vulnerability exposes customer systems
- Failed migration destroys customer data

**Custom Software Development**:
- Project delivered late, customer loses revenue opportunity
- Software doesn't meet specifications, customer demands refund + damages
- Integration with legacy systems fails, business processes disrupted
- Scope creep disputes and budget overruns

**IT Services & MSPs**:
- Failed backup results in client data loss
- Misconfigured firewall allows breach
- Patch management error takes down client systems
- Cloud migration loses data

**Example Claims**:
- **SaaS CRM outage** (48 hours): Customer loses $5M in sales, claims breach of SLA → **$3.5M settlement**
- **E-commerce platform bug**: Pricing error causes company to sell products at loss → **$1.8M claim**
- **Custom ERP implementation failure**: Software delivered 9 months late, doesn't work → **$6M lawsuit**
- **Managed security provider**: Misconfiguration allows ransomware attack on client → **$4.2M claim**

### E&O Policy Structure

**Claims-Made Coverage** (Standard for Tech E&O):
- Coverage triggered when claim is **made** (reported to insurer during policy period)
- Must maintain continuous coverage to avoid gaps
- Requires "tail" coverage (Extended Reporting Period) if policy is cancelled
- **Retroactive date**: Coverage only for acts after this date (usually first policy inception)

**Occurrence Coverage** (Rare for Tech):
- Coverage triggered when incident **occurred**, regardless of when claim is reported
- No tail needed, permanent coverage for policy period
- Much more expensive (30-50% higher premium) and hard to find

**Defense Costs**:
- Most policies provide defense **in addition to limits** (outside the limit)
- Some policies provide defense **within limits** (erodes coverage)
- "Duty to defend" policies: Insurer controls defense, you can't choose attorney
- "Right to defend" policies: You control defense with insurer approval

### E&O Pricing by Company Type

Premiums vary based on revenue, services, and risk exposure:

| Company Type | Revenue Range | Typical Limits | Annual Premium |
|--------------|--------------|----------------|----------------|
| **Early-stage SaaS** | <$1M | $1M-$2M | $5K-$15K |
| **Growth SaaS** | $1M-$10M | $2M-$5M | $15K-$50K |
| **Mid-market SaaS** | $10M-$100M | $5M-$10M | $50K-$150K |
| **Enterprise SaaS** | $100M+ | $10M-$25M | $150K-$500K |
| **Custom dev shop** | $1M-$10M | $1M-$5M | $10K-$40K |
| **IT services/MSP** | $5M-$50M | $2M-$10M | $25K-$100K |
| **FinTech** | $10M-$100M | $5M-$25M | $75K-$300K |
| **HealthTech** | $10M-$100M | $5M-$20M | $60K-$250K |

**Pricing Factors**:
- Annual revenue and customer count
- Type of services (high-risk: payments, healthcare, financial services)
- Customer contracts (B2B enterprise more expensive than B2C)
- SLA commitments (99.99% uptime = higher risk)
- Claims history (prior claims increase premiums 25-100%)
- Security posture (SOC 2, ISO 27001 can reduce premiums 10-25%)

### Reducing E&O Risk & Premiums

**Contractual Protections** (10-25% premium reduction):
- ✅ **Limitation of liability clauses**: Cap damages at 12 months fees or similar
- ✅ **Disclaimer of consequential damages**: Exclude lost profits, business interruption
- ✅ **SLA reasonable targets**: Don't promise 100% uptime (99.5%-99.9% is realistic)
- ✅ **Indemnification provisions**: Balanced indemnities, not unlimited
- ✅ **Warranty limitations**: Disclaim warranties beyond express guarantees

**Technical Controls** (10-30% premium reduction):
- ✅ **SOC 2 Type II certification**: Annual audit of security controls
- ✅ **ISO 27001 certification**: Information security management system
- ✅ **Change management process**: Documented, tested deployments
- ✅ **Quality assurance**: Automated testing, code reviews
- ✅ **Backup and disaster recovery**: Tested 3-2-1 backup strategy
- ✅ **Incident response plan**: Documented, tested annually

**Customer Selection**:
- Avoid extremely high-risk customers (gambling, cannabis, adult content)
- Limit exposure to any single customer (<20% of revenue)
- Avoid mission-critical applications without adequate insurance

## Cyber & Data Breach Liability

### Why Tech Companies Are Prime Targets

**Technology companies face disproportionate cyber risk**:
- Store vast amounts of customer data (credentials, PII, payment info)
- High-value targets for nation-state actors and organized crime
- Supply chain attacks (compromise one SaaS vendor, access hundreds of customers)
- Customer trust is brand reputation (breach = customer exodus)

**Statistics**:
- **60%** of tech companies experience a cyberattack annually
- **Average breach cost**: $4.88M (tech sector)
- **SaaS platform breach**: Can expose data for thousands of customers simultaneously
- **Ransomware targeting**: 75% of ransomware attacks target <100 employee companies

### Cyber Insurance Coverage

**First-Party Coverages** (Your Direct Costs):
- **Breach Response & Notification**: Forensics, legal, notification, credit monitoring ($500K-$5M)
- **Data Restoration**: Rebuild systems after ransomware or destruction ($250K-$2M)
- **Ransomware Payment**: Pay extortion demand with negotiation support ($100K-$1M sublimit, 20% coinsurance)
- **Business Interruption**: Lost revenue from system downtime ($1M-$25M)
- **Cyber Extortion**: Threats to release data or launch DDoS ($250K-$1M)
- **System Failure BI**: Non-malicious outages (hardware, human error) ($1M-$10M)
- **Reputational Harm**: PR and crisis management ($100K-$1M)
- **Regulatory Fines**: GDPR, CCPA penalties ($500K-$5M)

**Third-Party Coverages** (Claims Against You):
- **Privacy Liability**: Lawsuits from individuals whose data was breached ($1M-$25M)
- **Network Security Liability**: Harm to others from your compromised systems ($1M-$25M)
- **Media Liability**: Defamation, copyright infringement from digital content ($1M-$5M)
- **PCI-DSS Fines**: Payment card industry penalties and assessments ($100K-$1M)
- **Regulatory Defense**: Legal costs defending against regulatory actions ($500K-$5M)

### Cyber Limits by Company Type

| Company Type | Recommended Limits | Typical Premium |
|--------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Startup (<$5M revenue)** | $2M-$5M | $8K-$25K |
| **Growth stage ($5M-$25M)** | $5M-$15M | $25K-$80K |
| **Mid-market ($25M-$100M)** | $10M-$30M | $75K-$250K |
| **Enterprise ($100M+)** | $25M-$100M | $250K-$1M+ |
| **Cloud/SaaS w/ customer data** | $15M-$50M | $100K-$500K |
| **FinTech/HealthTech** | $25M-$100M+ | $200K-$800K |

**Higher limits needed if**:
- Large customer databases (>1M records)
- High-value customer data (financial, health, credentials)
- Enterprise B2B customers (class action risk)
- International operations (GDPR fines up to 4% of global revenue)
- Payment processing (PCI-DSS fines and card replacement costs)

### Cyber Insurance Requirements (Get Coverage!)

**Baseline Controls** (Required by Most Insurers):
- ✅ **Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)**: On all admin accounts, email, and remote access (MANDATORY)
- ✅ **Endpoint Detection & Response (EDR)**: Advanced antivirus on all devices
- ✅ **Offline Backups**: Air-gapped, immutable backups tested quarterly
- ✅ **Patch Management**: Critical vulnerabilities patched within 30 days
- ✅ **Email Security**: Anti-phishing (DMARC, SPF, DKIM)
- ✅ **Privileged Access Management**: Least privilege, no shared admin accounts

**Without These, Expect**:
- Coverage denial (no MFA = no cyber insurance in 2025)
- 50-100% premium surcharges
- Higher deductibles ($100K-$250K vs. $25K-$50K)
- Exclusions for ransomware or specific attack vectors

### Notable Cyber Claims in Tech

**Real-World Examples**:
- **SaaS HR platform breach** (2023): 29M records exposed → $12M in notification, credit monitoring, legal defense, regulatory fines
- **Cloud backup provider ransomware** (2024): Customer data encrypted → $8M ransom paid + $15M in recovery costs
- **Payment processor breach** (2023): 15M payment cards compromised → $45M in PCI fines, card reissuance, fraud losses
- **AI chatbot company** (2024): Training data leaked PII → $6M GDPR fine + $10M class action settlement

## Intellectual Property Protection

### IP Risks for Tech Companies

**Common IP Exposures**:
- **Patent infringement**: Your software allegedly uses patented algorithms or methods
- **Copyright infringement**: Code, UI/UX, or content copied from others
- **Trademark disputes**: Brand name or logo conflicts
- **Trade secret theft**: Competitors or ex-employees steal proprietary information
- **Open source violations**: GPL or other copyleft license breaches

### IP Defense Coverage

**What It Covers**:
- ✅ Defense costs when accused of IP infringement
- ✅ Settlement or judgment costs
- ✅ Legal fees for patent, copyright, trademark litigation
- ✅ Licensing negotiations after infringement claim

**What It Doesn't Cover**:
- ❌ Offensive IP litigation (suing others for infringement) - separate cost
- ❌ Patent application costs
- ❌ Known infringement before policy inception

**Typical Limits**: $1M-$5M per claim, $2M-$10M aggregate  
**Premium**: $10K-$50K annually (varies by patent portfolio and industry)

### Patent Troll Insurance

**Non-Practicing Entities (NPEs)** are a major risk:
- **Patent trolls**: Buy patents, sue tech companies for licensing fees
- Don't produce products, just monetize patents through litigation
- **Cost to defend**: $2M-$5M even if you win
- **Settlement pressure**: Often cheaper to settle ($100K-$500K) than fight

**Patent Defense Insurance**:
- Covers defense against NPE patent claims
- Limits: $5M-$15M
- Premium: $15K-$60K (depending on company size and sector)
- Some policies include **adverse judgment coverage** (pay damages if you lose)

### IP Offense Coverage (Rare)

**If You Need to Sue Others**:
- Very expensive ($10M+ in legal fees for major patent litigation)
- Insurance available but costly ($50K-$200K premium)
- Usually structured as **portfolio** coverage for multiple patents
- Often requires winning before insurer pays (contingent)

### Open Source Compliance

**Open Source Risks**:
- **GPL violations**: Using GPL code in proprietary software without releasing source
- **License incompatibilities**: Mixing Apache, MIT, GPL code incorrectly
- **Supply chain**: Third-party libraries with hidden copyleft licenses

**Best Practices**:
- Software Composition Analysis (SCA) tools (Snyk, Black Duck, WhiteSource)
- Open source policy and training for developers
- Legal review of all third-party dependencies
- Audit before funding rounds or M&A (due diligence will uncover violations)

## Directors & Officers (D&O) for Tech Companies

### Why Tech Companies Need D&O

**High-Risk Events**:
- **Securities litigation**: Stock price drops after earnings miss, restatement, or scandal
- **Failed IPO/SPAC**: Investors sue for misrepresentation or omissions
- **M&A disputes**: Shareholders claim directors breached fiduciary duty in sale
- **Employment claims**: Discrimination, harassment, wrongful termination (against executives)
- **Regulatory actions**: SEC, FTC, DOJ investigations
- **Shareholder derivative suits**: Waste of corporate assets, self-dealing

**Who's At Risk**:
- **Directors and Officers**: Board members, C-suite executives
- **Individual liability**: Personal assets at risk if no D&O coverage
- **Company liability**: Entity coverage for indemnification obligations

### D&O Coverage Structure

**Side A** (Individual Coverage):
- Pays directors/officers when company **cannot** indemnify them
- Bankruptcy, insolvency, or legally barred from indemnifying
- Most important coverage (protects personal assets)

**Side B** (Company Reimbursement):
- Reimburses company for indemnification payments to directors/officers
- Company indemnifies D&Os per bylaws, insurance reimburses company

**Side C** (Entity/Securities Coverage):
- Covers the company itself in securities litigation
- Only available for public companies or those in registration
- Protects corporate assets

### D&O Limits by Stage

| Company Stage | Typical Limits | Annual Premium |
|---------------|----------------|----------------|
| **Seed/Pre-Series A** | $1M-$3M | $5K-$15K |
| **Series A-B** | $5M-$15M | $15K-$50K |
| **Series C+** | $15M-$50M | $50K-$200K |
| **Pre-IPO** | $50M-$150M | $200K-$800K |
| **Public (small cap)** | $25M-$100M | $150K-$500K |
| **Public (mid cap)** | $50M-$250M | $500K-$2M |
| **Public (large cap)** | $100M-$500M+ | $2M-$10M+ |

**D&O Pricing Factors**:
- Company valuation and burn rate
- Funding stage (later stage = higher premium)
- Revenue and profitability (losses = higher risk)
- Board composition (experienced directors reduce risk)
- Corporate governance (audit committee, independent directors)
- Prior litigation or SEC investigations
- Industry (FinTech, HealthTech, Cannabis = higher premiums)

### Critical D&O Considerations for Tech

**Pre-IPO**:
- **Increase limits** significantly 6-12 months before IPO ($50M-$150M)
- Add **Side C** entity coverage (not needed pre-IPO)
- **Tail coverage**: 6-year extended reporting period (statutes of limitations)
- Expect 3-10x premium increase for public company D&O

**M&A**:
- **Run-off policy** (tail) for acquired company's D&Os (6-year typical)
- Cost: 200-300% of final annual premium (one-time)
- Buyer usually pays for seller's run-off as part of deal

**Down Rounds & Restructuring**:
- High-risk events for shareholder litigation
- Ensure adequate Side A limits (company may not be able to indemnify)
- Consider increasing coverage before announcing bad news

**Employee vs. Executive Coverage**:
- D&O covers **officers** (VP-level and above typically)
- EPLI covers **employees** (wrongful termination, discrimination)
- Overlap in middle (VPs may be covered by both)

## Product Liability for Software & Hardware

### Software Product Liability

**Traditionally, software was NOT subject to product liability** (intangible good, not "product"). But this is changing:

**Emerging Risks**:
- **Autonomous vehicles**: Software failure causes accident
- **Medical devices**: FDA-regulated software as a medical device (SaMD)
- **Critical infrastructure**: Software controlling power grids, water systems
- **AI/ML systems**: Algorithmic harm (biased decisions, faulty recommendations)

**Coverage**:
- **Tech E&O** typically covers software failures (primary coverage)
- **Product Liability** may be needed for:
  - Embedded software in physical products (IoT, automotive, medical)
  - SaaS controlling critical systems (manufacturing, energy)
  - AI making high-stakes decisions (hiring, lending, healthcare)

### Hardware Product Liability

**For Companies Selling Physical Products**:
- **Laptops, servers, networking equipment**: Fire, electrical shock hazards
- **Consumer electronics**: Battery fires, overheating
- **IoT devices**: Security vulnerabilities, physical harm
- **Robotics**: Physical injury from mechanical failure

**Coverage**:
- **General Liability** includes products-completed operations
- **Product Recall Coverage**: Costs to recall defective products (sublimit or separate)
- Limits: $2M-$10M (consumer products), $10M-$50M (critical systems)

**Product Recall Costs**:
- Notification (FTC, CPSC, customers)
- Return shipping and logistics
- Refunds or replacement units
- Disposal of defective inventory
- Crisis management and PR

**Example**:
- **IoT security camera recall** (2023): 500K units, vulnerability allowed remote access → $8M recall costs (notification, return shipping, replacements, PR)

## Media Liability

### Coverage for Digital Content

**What Media Liability Covers**:
- ✅ **Defamation**: Libel, slander in user-generated content or company communications
- ✅ **Copyright infringement**: Using others' content (images, videos, text) without permission
- ✅ **Trademark infringement**: Using others' trademarks in content or advertising
- ✅ **Privacy violations**: Using someone's likeness without consent
- ✅ **Idea theft**: Misappropriation of ideas from unsolicited submissions
- ✅ **Advertising injury**: False advertising, misrepresentation

**Who Needs It**:
- Social media platforms (user-generated content)
- Content publishers (blogs, news sites, video platforms)
- Marketing/advertising agencies
- Gaming companies (user chat, forums)
- E-commerce (product descriptions, user reviews)

**Typical Limits**: $1M-$5M  
**Premium**: $3K-$30K annually

**Often Included In**:
- Tech E&O policies (as sublimit or endorsement)
- Cyber insurance (as "media liability" section)
- General Liability (advertising injury coverage, but limited)

### Section 230 (U.S. Protection)

**CDA Section 230** protects platforms from liability for user-generated content:
- Not liable for content posted by users
- Can moderate content without losing protection
- **But**: Doesn't protect against IP infringement (DMCA applies)
- **International**: No Section 230 equivalent in most countries (EU DSA has takedown requirements)

## Cloud & Data Center Risks

### Service Interruption Coverage

**Business Interruption for SaaS**:
- **Trigger**: Platform outage, data center failure, DDoS attack
- **Coverage**: Lost revenue during downtime
- **Waiting Period**: Typically 8-24 hours (deductible in time)
- **Period of Restoration**: 30-90 days typical
- **Limits**: $1M-$25M (match your monthly/quarterly revenue)

**Extra Expense**:
- Emergency cloud capacity (surge pricing)
- Expedited equipment or bandwidth
- Customer communication and support
- PR and crisis management

**Dependent Business Interruption**:
- Loss from **supplier** failure (AWS outage, CDN failure, payment processor down)
- Critical for companies dependent on third-party infrastructure
- Often excluded or limited sublimit ($250K-$1M)

### Data Center Physical Risks

**For Companies with On-Prem Infrastructure**:
- **Property Coverage**: Servers, networking, HVAC, generators
- **Equipment Breakdown**: Mechanical/electrical failure
- **Business Interruption**: Lost revenue during equipment failure
- **Data Restoration**: Rebuild data from physical destruction

**Typical Limits**:
- Property: $1M-$10M (replacement cost of equipment)
- BI: 6-12 months revenue
- Equipment Breakdown: $1M-$5M

### Service Level Agreement (SLA) Insurance

**Contractual Liability Coverage**:
- Covers **penalties** for SLA breaches (uptime, performance)
- Usually excluded from E&O (contractual guarantees)
- Available as endorsement or separate policy
- Limits: $500K-$5M
- Premium: 1-3% of limit

**Example**:
- SaaS company guarantees 99.9% uptime (8.7 hours downtime/year allowed)
- Outage: 72 hours → Contractual penalties: $2M
- SLA insurance pays $2M penalty

## Employment Practices Liability

### Why Tech Companies Face EPLI Claims

**High-Risk Factors**:
- **Rapid growth**: Hiring fast → poor training, weak HR processes
- **Startup culture**: Informal policies, work-life boundary issues
- **Young workforce**: Less experienced managers, peer conflicts
- **High-stakes equity**: Terminations involving unvested stock options
- **Gender diversity issues**: Tech industry has documented bias problems

**Common EPLI Claims**:
- **Wrongful termination**: Fired employee claims discrimination or retaliation
- **Discrimination**: Age, race, gender, disability claims
- **Sexual harassment**: Hostile work environment, quid pro quo
- **Retaliation**: Whistleblower retaliation, complaint retaliation
- **Failure to promote**: Passed over for promotion, claims bias
- **Wage and hour**: Misclassification, unpaid overtime, meal break violations

### EPLI Coverage

**What's Covered**:
- ✅ Defense costs (attorneys, expert witnesses, settlements)
- ✅ Settlements and judgments
- ✅ Administrative proceedings (EEOC, state labor agencies)
- ✅ Third-party claims (customers, vendors alleging harassment)

**What's NOT Covered**:
- ❌ Wage and hour violations (often excluded or sublimit)
- ❌ WARN Act violations (mass layoff notifications)
- ❌ Intentional discrimination by executives
- ❌ Unemployment claims
- ❌ Workers compensation

**Typical Limits**: $1M-$10M  
**Premium**: $3K-$50K (varies by employee count, industry)

### EPLI Pricing

| Employee Count | Typical Limits | Annual Premium |
|----------------|----------------|----------------|
| **<25 employees** | $1M | $3K-$8K |
| **25-100** | $2M | $8K-$20K |
| **100-500** | $5M | $20K-$50K |
| **500-1,000** | $5M-$10M | $50K-$100K |
| **1,000+** | $10M+ | $100K-$300K+ |

**Reducing EPLI Risk**:
- ✅ Written HR policies (handbook, anti-harassment policy)
- ✅ Annual harassment training (managers and employees)
- ✅ Documented performance reviews (progressive discipline)
- ✅ Consistent termination process (HR involvement, exit interviews)
- ✅ Background checks and reference checks
- ✅ Employment counsel review (high-risk terminations)

## Crime & Social Engineering

### Crime Coverage for Tech

**What Crime Insurance Covers**:
- ✅ **Employee theft**: Embezzlement, fraud by employees
- ✅ **Social engineering**: Business email compromise (BEC), CEO fraud
- ✅ **Funds transfer fraud**: Fraudulent wire transfers, ACH fraud
- ✅ **Computer fraud**: Hacking of your systems to steal funds
- ✅ **Forgery**: Forged checks or payment instructions
- ✅ **Credit card fraud**: Fraudulent use of company cards

**Typical Limits**: $1M-$10M  
**Premium**: $2K-$15K annually

### Business Email Compromise (BEC)

**Most Common Tech Crime**:
- Hacker compromises email account or spoofs executive email
- Sends fraudulent wire transfer instructions
- Finance team sends money to attacker's account
- **Average loss**: $180K per incident

**Example**:
- CFO's email hacked while traveling
- Attacker sends email to controller: "Urgent: Wire $450K to this account for acquisition"
- Controller wires funds → Discovers fraud 2 days later
- **Recovery**: $0 (funds already moved through multiple international accounts)
- **Crime insurance**: Pays $450K (within limits)

**Prevention**:
- ✅ Dual authorization for wires >$50K
- ✅ Verbal verification (call known phone number, not number in email)
- ✅ Email authentication (DMARC, DKIM, SPF)
- ✅ Training on BEC tactics (sense of urgency, executive impersonation)

## Insurance for Startups vs. Scale-Ups

### Early Stage (Pre-Seed to Series A)

**Minimum Coverage**:
- General Liability: $1M/$2M ($2K-$5K)
- Tech E&O: $1M ($5K-$15K)
- Cyber Liability: $1M-$2M ($8K-$20K)
- D&O: $1M-$5M ($5K-$25K)
- Workers Comp: Statutory (if employees)

**Total Annual Cost**: $20K-$60K

**Why These Coverages**:
- **GL**: Required for office lease, events
- **Tech E&O**: Required by enterprise customers (RFPs demand $1M-$2M)
- **Cyber**: Increasingly required by customers and investors
- **D&O**: Protects founders and board members (VCs often require)

### Growth Stage (Series B-C)

**Enhanced Coverage**:
- General Liability: $2M/$4M ($5K-$10K)
- Tech E&O: $5M ($25K-$75K)
- Cyber Liability: $10M-$25M ($50K-$150K)
- D&O: $15M-$50M ($50K-$200K)
- EPLI: $5M ($15K-$40K)
- Crime: $5M ($5K-$15K)

**Total Annual Cost**: $150K-$490K

**Why Increase Limits**:
- Larger customers demand higher limits
- More employees = more EPLI exposure
- Higher valuation = bigger targets for litigation
- International expansion = GDPR and global regulations

### Scale-Up/Pre-IPO (Series D+)

**Comprehensive Program**:
- General Liability: $5M primary + $25M umbrella ($25K-$75K)
- Tech E&O: $10M-$25M ($100K-$300K)
- Cyber Liability: $25M-$100M ($200K-$600K)
- D&O: $75M-$150M ($250K-$800K)
- EPLI: $10M ($40K-$80K)
- Crime: $10M ($10K-$25K)
- Fiduciary Liability: $10M (if ESOP or 401k) ($5K-$15K)

**Total Annual Cost**: $630K-$1.9M

**Additional Considerations**:
- **IPO preparation**: D&O limits need 5-10x increase
- **M&A activity**: Transaction liability insurance for acquisitions
- **International**: Local policies for foreign entities (UK, EU, APAC)
- **Captive insurance**: Self-insure part of risk to reduce costs

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What's the difference between Tech E&O and Cyber insurance?**

A: **Tech E&O** covers professional services and software failures (bugs, downtime, SLA breaches). **Cyber** covers data breaches, hacking, and privacy violations. Many companies need both. Some insurers combine them in a "Technology Package."

**Q: Do I need D&O before raising venture capital?**

A: **Yes, highly recommended.** VCs expect D&O coverage to protect board members. Many require it in investment terms. Cost is low for early-stage ($5K-$15K) and protects founders personally.

**Q: Can I get cyber insurance if I don't have MFA?**

A: **No, not in 2025.** MFA is mandatory for cyber insurance. Insurers won't cover you without MFA on all admin accounts and email. Implement MFA first (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, Okta, Duo).

**Q: What if a customer requires $10M E&O but I only have $2M?**

A: Options:
1. **Increase your policy limits** (costs 2-3x premium for 5x limits)
2. **Project-specific policy** (just for that customer, one-time cost)
3. **Customer accepts lower limits** with strong contract terms (liability caps, disclaimer of consequential damages)

**Q: Does E&O cover me if my software has a security vulnerability?**

A: **Depends.** E&O covers software defects and failures. Cyber covers data breaches. If vulnerability causes customer data breach, **Cyber** applies. If vulnerability causes customer system failure (no breach), **E&O** applies. Get both.

**Q: I'm bootstrapped and insurance is expensive. What's essential?**

A: **Bare minimum**:
1. General Liability (required for leases, $2K-$5K)
2. Tech E&O (required by enterprise customers, $5K-$15K)
3. Cyber (increasingly required, $8K-$20K)

**Total: $15K-$40K**. Not optional if you have customers or employees.

**Q: What happens to my insurance when I get acquired?**

A: **Run-off tail coverage** (6-year typical):
- Covers claims made after acquisition for pre-acquisition acts
- Buyer usually pays for this (2-3x annual premium, one-time)
- Essential for D&O, E&O, EPLI

**Q: Do I need separate coverage for my API?**

A: **Usually no.** API failures covered under Tech E&O (part of your software/platform). If you sell API as standalone product to developers, ensure E&O policy explicitly covers API services.

**Q: Can I skip General Liability if I'm purely digital (no office, remote team)?**

A: **Risky.** GL is cheap ($2K-$5K) and covers more than premises liability:
- Advertising injury (IP claims in your marketing)
- Products-completed operations (if any physical goods)
- Defense costs for third-party claims

Landlords and event venues also require it.

**Q: What's the difference between patent defense and patent offense insurance?**

A: **Patent Defense**: Covers you when sued for infringement (someone claims you violated their patent). **Patent Offense**: Covers your costs to sue others (expensive, rare). Most companies only need defense coverage.
