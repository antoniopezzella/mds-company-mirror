# Insurance & Risk Management: Executive Perspectives

## Overview

Different executives view insurance and risk management through distinct lenses based on their organizational responsibilities. This guide addresses the unique concerns, priorities, and questions of key decision-makers: CFOs, Chief Risk Officers, CISOs, HR Leaders, and Operations Executives.

### CFO Questions Answered

**"How much should we spend on insurance?"**

Industry benchmarks vary, but typical ranges:
- Low-risk services (professional services, technology): 0.5-1.5% of revenue
- Moderate-risk operations (manufacturing, distribution): 1.5-3% of revenue
- High-risk operations (construction, healthcare): 3-7% of revenue

Factors affecting spend:
- Industry and business model
- Risk profile (loss history, operations, geography)
- Risk retention strategy (higher deductibles reduce premiums)
- Coverage breadth (bare minimum vs. comprehensive)

Marsh benchmarks your spend against peers to identify optimization opportunities.

**"What's our total cost of risk?"**

Total Cost of Risk = Insurance Premiums + Retained Losses + Risk Management Expenses + Indirect Costs

Components:
1. **Insurance Premiums**: Direct cost of risk transfer
2. **Retained Losses**: Deductibles, self-insured retentions, uninsured losses
3. **Risk Management**: Safety programs, loss control, captive administration
4. **Indirect Costs**: Claims administration, productivity losses, opportunity costs

Best-in-class organizations track TCOR quarterly and target continuous reduction through balanced risk transfer and mitigation.

**"Should we increase deductibles to reduce premiums?"**

Deductible analysis requires evaluating:
- **Premium Savings**: Typically 20-40% reduction for 5-10x higher deductible
- **Loss Frequency**: How often will deductible be hit? (claims history analysis)
- **Cash Flow Impact**: Can we absorb larger losses without financial distress?
- **Risk Appetite**: Board and management comfort with volatility

Example: Increasing property deductible from $25K to $250K might save $100K in premium but exposes you to $225K more per loss. If you average 2 claims/year, you're paying $450K more in deductibles to save $100K in premium—bad trade. But if claims are rare (0.2/year), math favors higher deductible.

Marsh models expected losses at various retentions to optimize deductible levels.

**"Is a captive insurance company right for us?"**

Captives make sense when:
- Premium spend exceeds $500K-$1M annually (minimum for feasibility)
- Stable, predictable loss history
- Desire to retain profits from favorable loss experience
- Coverage gaps in commercial market
- Tax optimization opportunities
- Access to reinsurance markets

Benefits:
- Reduce total cost of risk by 15-30% over 5-7 years
- Customize coverage for unique risks
- Smooth earnings volatility through reserves
- Profit from underwriting and investment income

Considerations:
- Upfront formation costs: $50K-$150K
- Annual administration: $75K-$200K
- Regulatory compliance and governance
- Capital requirements and collateral

Marsh Captive Solutions conducts feasibility studies to evaluate financial viability.

**"How do we quantify uninsurable risks?"**

Some risks lack insurance solutions (reputation, strategic, regulatory). Quantification approaches:

1. **Scenario Analysis**: Model financial impact of specific events (data breach, product recall, regulatory fine)
2. **Monte Carlo Simulation**: Probabilistic modeling of range of outcomes
3. **Value-at-Risk (VaR)**: Statistical measure of maximum expected loss at confidence level (95%, 99%)
4. **Enterprise Risk Management**: Aggregate all risks to understand total enterprise exposure

Quantification enables:
- Risk-informed decision making
- Capital reserves planning
- Board risk appetite discussions
- Prioritization of mitigation investments

**"What's the ROI of risk management programs?"**

Measuring ROI:
- **Cost Avoidance**: Losses prevented by safety programs, security controls, training
- **Premium Reduction**: Credits from carriers for loss control improvements
- **Productivity Gains**: Reduced downtime, fewer workplace injuries
- **Reputation Protection**: Avoided brand damage from incidents

Example: Investing $100K in cybersecurity (MFA, EDR, training) may:
- Reduce cyber premiums by 20% ($50K savings on $250K premium)
- Avoid one ransomware incident every 3 years ($500K average cost)
- Expected annual value: $50K + $167K = $217K
- ROI: 117%

### CRO Questions Answered

**"How does insurance fit into our ERM framework?"**

Insurance is one of four risk treatment strategies:
1. **Avoid**: Eliminate risk exposure (exit business line, don't pursue opportunity)
2. **Mitigate**: Reduce likelihood or impact (safety programs, controls, redundancy)
3. **Transfer**: Shift risk to third party (insurance, contracts, outsourcing)
4. **Accept**: Retain risk within appetite (self-insure, contingency plans)

Insurance typically addresses:
- **High-severity, low-frequency risks**: Where single event could be catastrophic (property damage, liability lawsuits, cyber breach)
- **Mandatory coverage**: Workers compensation, auto liability, professional liability (for certain industries)
- **Contractual requirements**: Vendor agreements often require specific coverage and limits
- **Residual risk**: After mitigation, transfer remaining exposure

ERM identifies and prioritizes all risks; insurance addresses subset that are transferable and exceed risk appetite.

**"What risks can't be insured?"**

Common uninsurable or difficult-to-insure risks:
- **Strategic risks**: Business model disruption, competitive pressures, M&A integration
- **Reputation risk**: Brand damage, customer trust, social media backlash (some coverage for insurable events)
- **Regulatory/compliance risk**: Fines often excluded; defense costs may be covered
- **Known losses**: Pre-existing conditions, pending litigation
- **Market risks**: Currency fluctuations, commodity prices, interest rates (some parametric solutions)
- **War & terrorism**: Often excluded or limited coverage

For uninsurable risks, focus on:
- **Prevention & Mitigation**: Controls, monitoring, contingency plans
- **Reserves & Contingencies**: Financial cushion for unexpected events
- **Crisis Management**: Rapid response to minimize damage
- **Risk Monitoring**: Early warning indicators

**"How should we set risk appetite and tolerances?"**

Risk appetite framework defines how much risk organization willing to accept:

**Quantitative Thresholds**:
- Maximum expected loss (MEL) for single event: $X million
- Total cost of risk as % of revenue: <X%
- Retained risk (deductibles, SIRs): Up to $X per occurrence
- Aggregate retained losses: Cap at $X annually

**Qualitative Statements**:
- Zero tolerance for: Fraud, safety violations, ethics breaches
- Low appetite for: Reputation damage, regulatory violations
- Moderate appetite for: Innovation risks, strategic initiatives
- Higher appetite for: Market risks, operational efficiencies

**Risk Tolerance Levels**:
- **Green**: Within appetite, no escalation required
- **Yellow**: Approaching tolerance, monitoring and mitigation plans required
- **Red**: Exceeds tolerance, immediate action and executive approval needed

Board approves risk appetite; management implements through policies and controls.

**"How do we measure and report on risk?"**

Effective risk reporting includes:

**Risk Inventory & Register**:
- Comprehensive list of all identified risks
- Assessment of likelihood and impact (heat maps)

**Key Risk Indicators (KRIs)**:
- Leading indicators of emerging risks (early warnings)
- Lagging indicators of realized risks (loss trends)
- Thresholds triggering escalation
- Dashboard for executive/board consumption

**Scenario Analysis & Stress Testing**:
- "What if" scenarios (cyber breach, natural disaster, economic recession)
- Financial impact quantification
- Readiness and response capability assessment

**Insurance Program Performance**:
- Total cost of risk trends
- Claims frequency and severity
- Coverage adequacy and gaps
- Market conditions and outlook

**"How do we ensure insurance coverage is adequate as we grow?"**

Coverage adequacy review checklist:

**Annual Review Triggers**:
- Revenue growth exceeding 20%
- Acquisitions or divestitures
- New products, services, or business lines
- International expansion
- Significant capital investments (property, equipment)
- Leadership or organizational changes

**Coverage Adequacy Metrics**:
- Limit adequacy: Coverage limits vs. maximum expected loss and worst-case scenarios
- Sub-limits: Ensure adequate for specific exposures (cyber, flood, terrorism)
- Deductibles: Confirm organization can absorb without financial distress
- Policy triggers: Understand conditions for coverage (occurrence vs. claims-made)
- Exclusions: Identify gaps and evaluate whether acceptable or require endorsements

### CISO Questions Answered

**"What security controls do insurers require?"**

Cyber insurance underwriters assess security posture; many require:

**Mandatory Controls** (for most insureds):
- Multi-factor authentication (MFA) on all remote access and privileged accounts
- Endpoint detection and response (EDR) on all devices
- Regular patching and vulnerability management
- Offsite, encrypted backups tested for restoration
- Email security (anti-phishing, DMARC, SPF, DKIM)
- Privileged access management (PAM)

**Risk-Based Controls** (for larger or higher-risk insureds):
- Security Information and Event Management (SIEM)
- 24/7 Security Operations Center (SOC) or managed detection and response (MDR)
- Network segmentation and zero-trust architecture
- Incident response plan with tabletop exercises
- Cyber risk assessment and penetration testing
- Security awareness training (phishing simulations)

Failure to implement required controls may result in:
- Coverage denial
- Reduced limits
- Higher premiums
- Exclusions (e.g., ransomware sublimit)

**"How does cyber insurance complement our security program?"**

Cyber insurance is **not a substitute** for security; it's a financial safety net when prevention fails.

**What Cyber Insurance Covers**:
- Incident response costs (forensics, legal, crisis management)
- Ransomware negotiation and payment

- Business interruption from cyber events

**What It Doesn't Cover**:

- Cost to improve security after breach (betterments)

**Strategic Value**:

**"How should we handle ransomware?"**

**Prevention**:
- MFA on all access points
- EDR/XDR for endpoint protection
- Network segmentation to limit lateral movement
- Regular patching and vulnerability management
- Email filtering and anti-phishing
- Privileged access management
- Security awareness training

**Preparation**:
- Incident response plan with ransomware playbook
- Pre-vetted forensics, legal, crisis management vendors
- Offsite, encrypted backups tested quarterly
- Tabletop exercises simulating ransomware scenarios
- Cyber insurance with ransomware coverage

**Response**:
1. **Contain**: Isolate affected systems immediately
2. **Assess**: Determine scope, data exfiltrated, backups viability
3. **Notify**: Insurance carrier, legal counsel, forensics team
4. **Negotiate**: Experienced negotiators (often via insurance) engage attackers
5. **Recover**: Restore from backups if possible; decrypt if ransom paid
6. **Improve**: Post-incident review and security enhancements

**To Pay or Not to Pay?**
- FBI discourages payment (funds criminal enterprises)
- Reality: 50-70% of victims pay
- Decision factors: Backup viability, business criticality, regulatory implications, ransom amount
- Cyber insurance often covers ransom (subject to sublimits and deductibles)

Marsh coordinates response, including claims filing, vendor activation, and negotiation support.

**"How does workers compensation work, and how can we reduce costs?"**

Workers compensation is state-mandated insurance covering:
- Medical expenses for work-related injuries/illnesses
- Lost wages during recovery (typically 2/3 of wages)
- Rehabilitation and vocational training
- Disability benefits (temporary or permanent)
- Death benefits for families

**Workers Comp is No-Fault**: Employees receive benefits regardless of who caused injury; in exchange, they generally cannot sue employer.

**Cost Drivers**:
- Experience modification rate (EMR): Compares your claims to industry average (EMR > 1.0 = higher costs)
- Industry classification codes: Riskier industries pay higher rates
- Payroll: Premium calculated as rate per $100 of payroll
- State regulations: Rates and benefits vary by state

**Cost Reduction Strategies**:

**Loss Prevention**:
- Safety programs and training
- Ergonomics and workplace design
- Pre-employment screenings (within ADA limits)
- Drug-free workplace programs (premium credits in many states)
- Incident investigation and root cause analysis

**Claims Management**:
- Immediate reporting and medical care
- Modified duty/return-to-work programs (reduces wage replacement costs)
- Medical provider networks (lower costs, better outcomes)
- Fraud detection and investigation
- Proactive case management for serious injuries

**Program Design**:
- Deductible plans (retain small losses, save on premium)
- Retrospective rating (premium adjusted based on actual losses)
- Large deductible programs (self-insure up to $250K-$1M per claim)
- Captive insurance for very large employers

Reducing workers comp costs requires partnership between HR, safety, claims, and finance teams.

**"How do we calculate adequate business interruption limits?"**

BI insurance covers lost profits + continuing expenses (rent, salaries, utilities) during property damage recovery.

**Calculation Steps**:
1. **Historical Financials**: Analyze revenue, gross profit, fixed costs for past 2-3 years
2. **Recovery Period**: Estimate time to rebuild/repair (6-18 months typical; can be longer for catastrophic events)
3. **Indemnity Period**: Select policy period (12, 18, 24, 36 months)
4. **Gross Profit**: Revenue minus variable costs (cost of goods sold)
5. **Fixed Costs**: Expenses continuing during shutdown (payroll, rent, utilities, debt service)
6. **BI Limit**: (Gross Profit + Fixed Costs) x (Recovery Period / 12 months)

**Example**:
- Annual revenue: $50M
- Gross profit margin: 40% ($20M)
- Fixed costs: $10M
- Estimated recovery: 12 months
- BI Limit: $20M + $10M = $30M

**Common Mistakes**:
- Underestimating recovery time (supply chain delays, permitting, skilled labor shortages)
- Using net profit instead of gross profit + fixed costs
- Not including extra expenses (temporary facilities, expedited shipping)
- Failing to adjust limits as business grows

Marsh conducts BI adequacy reviews annually, modeling scenarios and recommending appropriate limits.

**Trade Credit Insurance**:
- Protects against customer insolvency or non-payment
- Enables safer credit extension and access to trade financing
- Covers domestic and international receivables

**Political Risk Insurance**:
- Expropriation, confiscation, nationalization
- Currency inconvertibility or non-transfer

**Operational Strategies**:

**"What's our exposure from equipment breakdown?"**

Equipment breakdown (EB) covers sudden, accidental failure of:
- Boilers, pressure vessels, HVAC systems
- Production machinery and manufacturing equipment
- Computer systems and servers
- Turbines, generators, transformers
- Refrigeration systems

**Why Standard Property Insurance Isn't Enough**:
- Property policies cover fire, theft, natural disasters—not mechanical/electrical failure
- EB fills gap for failures from wear, design defects, operator error

**Coverage Includes**:
- Repair or replacement of damaged equipment
- Business interruption from breakdown
- Expediting expenses (air freight parts, overtime labor, temporary equipment rental)
- Spoilage (food, pharmaceuticals requiring refrigeration)
- Ammonia contamination or hazardous substance cleanup

**Industries with High EB Exposure**:
- Manufacturing and industrial operations
- Food processing and cold storage
- Data centers and technology
- Healthcare (imaging equipment, sterilization)
- Hospitality (HVAC, elevators, kitchen equipment)

**Risk Mitigation**:
- Preventive maintenance programs
- Equipment inspections and testing
- Operator training and procedures
- Redundancy for critical systems
- Spare parts inventory

**"How do we prepare for natural disasters?"**

**Pre-Event Preparation**:

**Risk Assessment**:
- Identify exposures (hurricane, earthquake, flood, wildfire, tornado)
- Model probable maximum loss (PML) for each peril
- Prioritize facilities by criticality and risk

**Mitigation**:
- Building hardening (impact-resistant roofing, fire-resistant materials, flood barriers)
- Emergency power and water supplies
- Data backup and redundancy
- Equipment anchoring and seismic retrofits

**Business Continuity Plans**:
- Alternative operating locations
- Work-from-home capabilities
- Emergency communications plan
- Pre-arranged vendor agreements (restoration, temporary facilities)

**Insurance Optimization**:
- Adequate property and BI limits
- Understand sub-limits and special deductibles (named storm, earthquake, flood)
- Consider parametric insurance for rapid cash access
- Flood insurance (excluded from standard property policies)

**Post-Event Response**:

1. **Safety First**: Account for all personnel, evacuate if necessary
2. **Document Damage**: Photos, videos, detailed descriptions before cleanup
3. **Notify Insurer**: Report claim within 24-48 hours
4. **Emergency Mitigation**: Prevent further damage (tarps, board-up, water extraction)
5. **Inventory Losses**: Equipment, inventory, building damage
6. **Track BI Losses**: Lost revenue, extra expenses, recovery timeline
7. **Vendor Mobilization**: Restoration contractors, engineers, adjusters

**Marsh Catastrophe Response**:
- Dedicated CAT team for major events
- Pre-loss planning and modeling
- Post-loss claims advocacy and coordination
- Temporary location and vendor assistance

**Supply Chain Coverage**: Schedule key suppliers in CBI; consider parametric triggers for rapid response

**Equipment Breakdown**: Ensure comprehensive coverage including expediting expenses

**Natural Disaster Prep**: Invest in mitigation; understand sub-limits and deductibles for CAT perils

**Claims Readiness**: Pre-event planning (documentation, contacts, procedures) accelerates recovery
