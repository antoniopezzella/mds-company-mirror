# Emerging Risks & Future Trends

## Overview

The risk landscape is evolving rapidly due to technological advancement, climate change, geopolitical instability, and societal shifts. Traditional insurance products must adapt to address these emerging exposures. This guide explores key emerging risks, their insurance implications, and how Marsh helps organizations navigate uncertainty.

## Climate Risk & Environmental Exposures

### Physical Climate Risks

**Increasing Frequency & Severity**:
- More frequent hurricanes, wildfires, floods, and severe storms
- Property damage escalating 5-8% annually in high-risk regions
- Business interruption extending from weeks to months
- Supply chain disruptions from climate events globally

**Coverage Implications**:
- Higher property premiums in catastrophe-prone areas (coastal, wildfire zones)
- Lower sub-limits for wind, flood, and wildfire
- Named storm deductibles (2-5% of insured value)
- Coverage restrictions or non-renewals in high-risk areas
- Flood insurance through NFIP or private markets (standard property policies exclude flood)

**Risk Mitigation Strategies**:
- Building hardening (impact-resistant roofing, fire-resistant materials, flood barriers)
- Business continuity planning with alternative locations
- Supply chain diversification geographically
- Parametric insurance for rapid cash access post-catastrophe
- Pre-event preparation contracts (restoration vendors on retainer)

### Transition Risks (Moving to Low-Carbon Economy)

**Business Model Disruption**:
- Stranded assets (fossil fuel reserves, combustion infrastructure)
- Regulatory changes (carbon taxes, emissions mandates, disclosure requirements)
- Market shifts (investor ESG preferences, consumer demand for sustainable products)
- Technology displacement (EV adoption, renewable energy, electrification)

**Insurance Considerations**:
- D&O liability for climate-related disclosures (SEC, investor lawsuits)
- Professional liability for ESG consultants and rating agencies
- Business interruption from supply chain partners unable to meet sustainability standards
- Reputation damage from greenwashing claims

### ESG & Sustainability

**Disclosure Requirements**:
- SEC climate disclosure rules (Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions)
- EU Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD)
- Task Force on Climate-Related Financial Disclosures (TCFD)
- Investor and stakeholder pressure for transparency

**Insurance Support**:
- D&O coverage for securities claims related to climate disclosures
- Professional liability for sustainability consultants
- Environmental liability for remediation and pollution
- Parametric coverage for renewable energy assets
- Supply chain insurance protecting against ESG non-compliance

**Marsh Capabilities**:

- Sustainability-linked insurance programs (premium discounts for ESG improvements)

## Supply Chain Disruption

### Modern Supply Chain Vulnerabilities

**Globalization & Complexity**:
- Just-in-time inventory leaving no buffer for disruptions
- Single-source suppliers creating concentration risk
- Long lead times for critical components (semiconductors, specialty materials)
- Geopolitical tensions affecting trade routes and tariffs

**Pandemic Lessons**:
- COVID-19 demonstrated fragility of global supply chains
- Factory shutdowns, port congestion, logistics bottlenecks
- Business interruption insurance gaps (communicable disease exclusions)
- Contingent business interruption often inadequate

**Coverage Solutions**:
- Contingent business interruption (CBI) with adequate limits and extended periods
- Parametric triggers for specific events (port closures, supplier bankruptcy)
- Trade credit insurance for receivables protection
- Political risk insurance for expropriation, currency inconvertibility
- Cargo and logistics insurance with broad coverage

### Supply Chain Risk Management

**Visibility & Mapping**:
- Map full supply chain including tier-2 and tier-3 suppliers
- Identify single points of failure and concentration risks
- Establish supplier financial health monitoring
- Scenario planning for major disruptions

**Diversification Strategies**:
- Multi-source critical components from different geographies
- Near-shoring or reshoring production for strategic goods
- Safety stock and buffer inventory for long lead-time items
- Alternative logistics routes and transportation modes

**Insurance Integration**:
- Schedule key suppliers in CBI coverage
- Include supplier default and insolvency extensions
- Ensure adequate time limits (12-18 months recovery, not 3-6 months)
- Consider parametric solutions for rapid cash flow

## Artificial Intelligence & Technology Risks

### AI-Specific Exposures

**Algorithmic Bias & Discrimination**:
- AI systems perpetuating or amplifying historical biases (hiring, lending, insurance pricing)
- Regulatory scrutiny (EU AI Act, state-level regulations)
- Class action lawsuits for discriminatory outcomes
- Reputation damage and customer attrition

**Coverage Gaps**:
- Traditional E&O may not cover AI-specific claims
- Cyber insurance excludes non-malicious AI failures
- Product liability for physical harm from AI decisions (autonomous vehicles, medical devices)
- IP infringement from AI-generated content or training data

**Emerging AI Insurance Products**:
- AI-specific professional liability
- Algorithmic bias and fairness coverage
- Model drift and performance degradation
- Intellectual property protection for AI training
- AI cyber extensions (adversarial attacks, data poisoning)

### Autonomous Systems

**Self-Driving Vehicles**:
- Shift from driver liability to manufacturer product liability
- Complex accident investigations (software vs. hardware vs. human)
- Regulatory uncertainty around liability frameworks
- Cybersecurity vulnerabilities

**Robotics & Automation**:
- Industrial robotics causing injury or property damage
- Service robots in healthcare, hospitality, retail
- Drone delivery and aerial surveillance
- Product liability evolving to software-driven decisions

**Insurance Adaptations**:
- Usage-based insurance (UBI) for autonomous vehicles
- Cyber-physical system coverage
- Product liability with software/firmware coverage
- Technology E&O for developers and manufacturers

### Deepfakes & Synthetic Media

**Emerging Threats**:
- CEO voice impersonation for wire fraud
- Fake videos damaging reputation or stock prices
- Synthetic identity fraud
- Disinformation campaigns targeting brands

**Coverage Needs**:
- Crime insurance extensions for deepfake-enabled fraud
- Cyber coverage for reputation harm from synthetic media
- D&O protection against securities manipulation
- Media liability for platforms hosting synthetic content

## Cyber Evolution

### Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS)

**Industrialization of Cybercrime**:
- Lower barrier to entry for attackers (subscription-based tools)
- More frequent, sophisticated, and damaging attacks
- Double and triple extortion (encrypt, exfiltrate, DDoS)
- Targeting of critical infrastructure (healthcare, energy, government)

**Coverage Trends**:
- Higher retentions for ransomware (carriers shifting risk to insureds)
- Security control requirements (MFA, EDR, backups, patching)
- Coverage sublimits for ransomware events
- Coinsurance provisions (insured shares 20-30% of loss)

### State-Sponsored Cyber Attacks

**Geopolitical Cyber Warfare**:
- Nation-state attacks on critical infrastructure
- Espionage targeting IP, trade secrets, government data
- Supply chain compromises (SolarWinds, NotPetya)
- Ambiguity between war and crime

**War Exclusions in Cyber Policies**:
- Debate over what constitutes "act of war" in cyber
- NotPetya ($10B losses) initially denied as war, later paid
- Industry moving toward "state-backed cyber operations" exclusions
- Affirmative coverage for non-war state-sponsored attacks

**Risk Mitigation**:
- Assume breach mindset (detection, response, recovery)
- Zero-trust architecture and network segmentation
- Threat intelligence sharing and monitoring
- Incident response plan with tabletop exercises
- Cyber insurance with clear war/terrorism exclusion language

### Quantum Computing Threat

**Cryptographic Vulnerability**:
- Quantum computers will break current encryption (RSA, ECC)
- "Harvest now, decrypt later" attacks collecting encrypted data
- Timeline: 5-15 years for cryptographically relevant quantum computers
- Transition to post-quantum cryptography required

**Business Impact**:
- Legacy encrypted data exposed (medical records, financial data, IP)
- Digital signatures and authentication compromised
- Blockchain and cryptocurrency vulnerabilities
- Long-term liability for data breaches occurring years earlier

**Preparation Steps**:
- Inventory encryption usage and data retention policies
- Monitor NIST post-quantum cryptography standards
- Plan migration to quantum-resistant algorithms
- Cyber insurance addressing quantum-enabled breaches

## Pandemic & Public Health Risks

### COVID-19 Lessons Learned

**Coverage Gaps Exposed**:
- Business interruption requires physical damage trigger (virus alone insufficient)
- Communicable disease exclusions added to most policies post-SARS
- Event cancellation insurance often excluded pandemics
- Supply chain and contingent business interruption inadequate

**Financial Impact**:
- $10+ trillion global economic losses from COVID-19
- Insured losses relatively small ($150B) due to exclusions
- Government intervention required (PPP loans, unemployment expansion)
- Uninsured losses borne by businesses and individuals

### Pandemic Risk Insurance Solutions

**Dedicated Pandemic Coverage**:
- Standalone pandemic business interruption policies
- Government-backed pandemic insurance programs (proposed in US, exists in UK)
- Parametric triggers (infection rates, hospitalizations, government lockdowns)
- Lower limits and higher premiums than traditional coverage

**Risk Mitigation Strategies**:
- Business continuity planning for remote work and operational resilience
- Supply chain diversification to reduce single-country dependencies
- Cash reserves for 6-12 months of operating expenses
- Event cancellation coverage with pandemic extensions (limited availability)

### Future Biological Risks

**Emerging Infectious Diseases**:

- Urbanization and human-wildlife interaction
- Antimicrobial resistance reducing treatment options
- Lab accidents or bioterrorism

**Long-Term Health Trends**:
- Aging populations increasing healthcare costs
- Chronic disease prevalence (diabetes, obesity)
- Mental health and substance abuse
- Impact on employee benefits and workers compensation

## Social Inflation

### Litigation Trends Driving Costs

**Nuclear Verdicts**:
- Jury awards exceeding $10M increasing 300% over past decade
- "Reptile theory" litigation tactics appealing to emotion over facts
- Third-party litigation funding enabling more lawsuits
- Social media influence on jury perceptions

**Examples**:
- Commercial auto: $90M verdict for delivery truck accident
- Product liability: $2B verdict for talc cancer claims
- Employment practices: $250M class action for discrimination

**Insurance Impact**:
- Commercial auto liability premiums up 30-50% in 5 years
- Higher retentions and coinsurance provisions
- Lower limits available from carriers
- Excess liability layers more expensive and restrictive

### Expanding Liability Theories

**Emerging Legal Doctrines**:
- Negligent hiring and supervision
- Failure to warn or adequately instruct
- Joint and several liability even with minimal fault
- Punitive damages for conduct deemed reckless

**New Liability Areas**:
- Failure to prevent cyberattacks (Target, Equifax)
- PFAS contamination (forever chemicals) liability
- Lead paint and asbestos analogs
- Failure to address climate change (carbon majors litigation)

### Risk Mitigation

**Defensive Strategies**:
- Enhanced driver screening and training programs
- Product warnings and instruction documentation
- Regular risk assessments and control improvements
- Crisis management and litigation response plans

**Insurance Structuring**:
- Higher primary limits to reduce claim frequency
- Carefully structured excess towers
- Aggregate limits consideration
- Alternative risk transfer (captives, self-insurance)

## Workforce Evolution

### Gig Economy & Contingent Workers

**Classification Challenges**:
- Independent contractor vs. employee determination
- AB5 in California and similar state laws
- DOL guidance and IRS scrutiny
- Misclassification penalties and back-taxes

**Coverage Implications**:
- Workers compensation excludes independent contractors (may create exposure if reclassified)
- Employment practices liability for classification disputes
- Contingent workers under general liability (may require endorsements)
- Cyber risk from contractors accessing systems

### Remote & Hybrid Work

**Property & Liability Shifts**:
- Home office equipment and cyber exposures
- Workers compensation for home injuries (jurisdictional variations)
- Auto liability when commuting less (usage-based insurance savings)
- Reduced commercial property values (less office space needed)

**Cybersecurity Challenges**:
- Home networks less secure than corporate
- Personal devices (BYOD) increasing attack surface
- Phishing more effective when working isolated
- Data protection across distributed workforce

**Coverage Adjustments**:
- Property schedules reflecting remote equipment
- Cyber insurance with work-from-home endorsements
- Employment practices for remote monitoring and privacy
- Commercial auto for reduced vehicle usage

### Mental Health & Wellbeing

**Rising Prevalence**:
- Anxiety, depression, burnout increasing post-pandemic
- Workers compensation mental health claims
- ADA accommodations for mental health conditions
- Employer duty of care expanding

**Insurance Trends**:
- Employee benefits adding mental health parity
- Workers compensation psychological injury claims
- Employment practices for failure to accommodate
- Disability insurance for mental health conditions

## Space & Satellite Risks

- Cyber risks to satellite operations

**Insurance Products**:

**New Risk Profile**:

- Cascading failures from debris
