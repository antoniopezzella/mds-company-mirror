# Client Journey & Processes

## Overview

Understanding what to expect when working with Marsh helps ensure a smooth partnership from initial consultation through renewal and claims. This guide outlines each stage of the client journey, typical timelines, deliverables, and how Marsh supports you throughout.

## Stage 1: Discovery & Prospecting

### What Happens

Initial exploration of whether Marsh is the right insurance partner for your organization.

**Activities**:
- Introductory meeting or call (30-60 minutes)
- Discussion of current insurance program and pain points
- Overview of Marsh capabilities and approach
- Preliminary assessment of potential value and savings
- Review of industry-specific expertise

**What We Need**:
- High-level overview of your business (industry, size, locations)
- Current insurance policies (declarations pages sufficient initially)
- Key concerns or recent changes (M&A, new products, international expansion)
- Decision-making process and timeline

**Timeline**: 1-2 meetings over 1-2 weeks

**Deliverables**:
- Initial assessment of coverage gaps or optimization opportunities
- Preliminary scope of services proposal
- Case studies or references from similar clients

### Common Questions at This Stage

**"What makes Marsh different from our current broker?"**
- Global capabilities with local expertise
- Proprietary data and analytics platforms
- Industry specialization and thought leadership
- Claims advocacy (we represent you, not carriers)
- Marsh McLennan ecosystem (consulting, talent, reinsurance)

**"Will we save money?"**
- Cost savings are possible but not guaranteed
- Focus is on optimizing total cost of risk (premiums + retained losses + risk management)
- Average clients see 10-20% improvements through market access, coverage optimization, and loss control

**"What's the transition process like?"**
- Typical transition: 60-90 days before renewal
- Minimal disruption to operations
- We coordinate with your current broker for smooth handoff

## Stage 2: Onboarding & Transition

### What Happens

Formal transition of insurance program to Marsh, typically timed to coincide with upcoming renewals.

**Activities**:
- Execute broker of record letter (authorizes Marsh to represent you)
- Comprehensive risk assessment and exposure analysis
- Review all current policies line-by-line
- Identify coverage gaps, redundancies, and optimization opportunities
- Build account team and communication protocols
- Establish technology access (client portal, certificates, claims systems)

**What We Need**:
- Complete current policy copies (not just declarations)
- Loss runs (5 years minimum, 10 years preferred)
- Schedule of values (property, equipment, locations)
- Completed insurance applications
- Organizational chart and key contacts
- Contracts requiring insurance (vendor agreements, leases)

**Timeline**: 4-8 weeks depending on complexity

**Deliverables**:
- Comprehensive risk assessment report
- Coverage analysis and gap evaluation
- Account team assignments and contact information
- Access to Marsh Digital client portal
- Service plan and communication schedule

### Transition Best Practices

**Start Early**: Begin 90-120 days before renewal for complex programs

**Centralize Information**: Designate a primary contact to coordinate information gathering

**Historical Context**: Share claims history, prior issues, and lessons learned

**Future Plans**: Inform us of M&A activity, new products, international expansion, or facility changes

### Common Concerns During Onboarding

**"Will our premiums increase during transition?"**
- We typically transition at renewal, maintaining continuity
- Market conditions (not broker change) drive premium movements
- Our goal is optimization, which may include restructuring for long-term savings

**"How much work is required from our team?"**
- Initial lift: 10-20 hours over 4-6 weeks (mostly information gathering)
- Ongoing: 2-5 hours per quarter for reviews
- We minimize disruption by doing heavy lifting ourselves

**"What if we're unhappy during transition?"**
- Open communication throughout
- Clearly defined milestones and success metrics
- 30-60 day checkpoints to assess satisfaction

## Stage 3: Marketing & Placement

### What Happens

Taking your insurance program to market to secure optimal coverage at competitive pricing.

**Activities**:
- Prepare comprehensive submission materials
- Select appropriate insurance carriers (target 3-7 per coverage line)
- Conduct underwriter meetings and facility tours if needed
- Negotiate terms, conditions, and pricing
- Conduct actuarial analysis for self-insurance and captives (if applicable)
- Prepare comparison analysis of proposals
- Make placement recommendations

**What We Need**:
- Any changes since initial underwriting (facilities, operations, revenue projections)
- Management discussion on risk appetite and retention preferences
- Budget parameters and coverage priorities
- Authority to bind coverage (or timeline for approvals)

**Timeline**: 
- Standard renewals: 30-60 days
- Complex programs: 60-90 days
- New coverage or high-hazard risks: 90-120 days

**Deliverables**:
- Market submission packages
- Carrier proposal summaries and comparisons
- Coverage recommendation memo
- Cost-benefit analysis of alternatives
- Binding authority and policy issuance

### Understanding the Process

**Market Selection**:
- We target carriers with appetite for your industry/risk profile
- Mix of incumbent and new markets for competitive tension
- Relationship leverage with carriers (volume, book quality)

**Underwriting**:
- Carriers evaluate your risk profile (financial strength, operations, loss history)
- Site visits may be requested for property or high-hazard operations
- Questionnaires specific to coverage type (cyber, D&O, professional liability)

**Negotiation**:
- Marsh negotiates on price, terms, conditions, and retention
- Industry benchmarking data supports negotiations
- Leverage relationships and volume for concessions

**Binding**:
- Once approved, we bind coverage to meet deadlines
- Policies issued 30-60 days post-binding
- Certificates of insurance available within 24 hours

### Common Questions About Marketing

**"How many carriers should quote?"**
- Standard: 3-5 markets per line
- High-hazard or tough risks: 5-10 markets
- Too many can dilute effort; we focus on quality markets

**"Should we keep incumbent carriers?"**
- Usually yes for competitive pressure
- Exception: Poor claims handling or consistent underperformance
- Carrier relationships matter for long-term stability

**"What if quotes come in higher than budget?"**
- Evaluate coverage reductions or higher retentions
- Multi-year agreements may lock in pricing
- Alternative risk transfer (captives, deductible programs)

## Stage 5: Renewal Process

### What Happens

Annual (or multi-year) renewal of insurance program with market testing and optimization.

**Activities**:
- 120-day planning and strategy session
- Update underwriting information and exposure data
- Conduct renewal market submission (typically 60-90 days before expiration)
- Benchmark current program against market
- Explore alternative structures or coverage enhancements
- Negotiate with incumbent and competing carriers
- Present renewal recommendations 30 days before expiration
- Bind coverage and ensure zero gaps in protection

**What We Need**:
- Updated financial information (revenue, payroll, property values)
- Schedule of locations, vehicles, equipment (as of renewal date)
- Description of new products, services, or operations
- Strategic plans affecting risk profile (M&A, international expansion)
- Budget parameters and coverage priorities
- Renewal meeting availability (plan for 1-2 meetings)

**Timeline**: 
- Simple renewals: 60 days
- Standard programs: 90 days
- Complex or challenged programs: 120+ days

**Deliverables**:
- Renewal strategy presentation
- Market comparison analysis
- Recommendation memo with rationale
- Renewed policy documents
- Updated certificates and evidence of insurance

### Renewal Strategy Considerations

**Incumbent Loyalty vs. Market Testing**:
- Stable programs with good claims: May renew with incumbent after market test
- Underperforming relationships: Aggressive market testing and likely carrier change
- Market-driven increases: Shop aggressively and consider multi-year deals

**Multi-Year Agreements**:
- Lock in pricing for 2-3 years
- Protection against hard market cycles
- Potential savings of 5-15% over term
- Requires stable risk profile

**Alternative Risk Transfer**:
- Higher deductibles to reduce premiums (20-40% savings possible)
- Captive insurance for large, stable organizations
- Parametric triggers for specific events (earthquakes, hurricanes)
- Risk retention groups for industry pools

### Common Renewal Questions

**"Why do premiums increase if we had no claims?"**
- Market conditions (carrier profitability, reinsurance costs, economic factors)
- Exposure growth (more revenue, locations, payroll increases premium base)
- Industry-wide loss trends (cyber, social inflation, natural catastrophes)

**"Can we switch carriers mid-policy?"**
- Generally not recommended (pro-rata return premiums, underwriting effort)
- Exception: Service failures, significant premium savings, or coverage gaps
- Some lines allow (professional liability, cyber); others don't (workers comp)

**"Should we stay with the same carrier long-term?"**
- Pros: Relationship credit, claims history understanding, stability
- Cons: Complacency, pricing creep, missing market opportunities
- Best practice: Test market every 2-3 years even if staying with incumbent

## Stage 6: Claims Management

### What Happens

When loss events occur, Marsh acts as your advocate throughout the claims process.

**Activities**:
- Immediate notification to carrier (Marsh assists with FNOL - First Notice of Loss)
- Assignment of adjuster and establishing claims strategy
- Regular status updates and timeline management
- Document and evidence gathering support
- Negotiation on coverage issues or payment disputes
- Settlement review and recommendation
- Post-claim analysis and loss prevention recommendations

**What We Need**:
- Immediate notification of loss (within 24-48 hours ideal)
- Detailed description of incident (who, what, when, where, how)
- Supporting documentation (photos, videos, police reports, medical records)
- Witness statements and contact information
- Relevant contracts or agreements
- Cooperation with adjuster (site access, interviews, records)

**Timeline**: 
- Simple property claims: 30-60 days
- Standard liability claims: 90-180 days
- Complex litigation: 1-5+ years
- Workers comp: Ongoing through resolution

**Deliverables**:
- Claims strategy and action plan
- Regular status updates (monthly minimum)
- Documentation of communications with carrier
- Settlement review and recommendation
- Post-loss analysis and prevention recommendations

### Types of Claims

**Property Claims (Fire, Theft, Natural Disaster)**:
- Immediate emergency services coordination
- Business interruption tracking and documentation
- Contents and equipment inventory
- Building damage assessment
- Temporary location assistance if needed

**Liability Claims (Injury, Damage, Lawsuits)**:
- Legal defense coordination
- Investigation and evidence preservation
- Settlement negotiation
- Trial support if litigation proceeds

**Workers Compensation**:
- Medical provider network guidance
- Return-to-work coordination
- Modified duty arrangements
- Disability and settlement negotiations

**Cyber Claims**:
- Incident response team activation (forensics, legal, PR)
- Regulatory notification coordination
- Ransom negotiation (if applicable)
- Business interruption quantification
- Credit monitoring for affected individuals

### Claims Best Practices

**Report Promptly**: Delays can jeopardize coverage; report within 24-48 hours of discovery

**Document Everything**: Photos, videos, emails, receipts - overcommunicate rather than under

**Don't Admit Fault**: Cooperate with investigation but reserve determinations for professionals

**Preserve Evidence**: Don't repair or dispose of damaged property before adjuster inspects

**Communicate Through Marsh**: We manage carrier communications and advocate for your interests

**Track Business Interruption**: Document lost revenue, extra expenses, and recovery timeline

### When Claims Get Complicated

**Coverage Disputes**:
- Marsh reviews policy language and advocates for broadest interpretation
- Engage coverage counsel if necessary
- Escalate within carrier organization
- Consider mediation or appraisal processes

**Settlement Disagreements**:
- Independent appraisals or expert opinions
- Market comparisons for replacement costs
- Demonstrate financial impact with documentation
- Negotiate persistently; early offers often low

**Subrogation Recovery**:
- If third party caused loss, carrier may pursue recovery
- Marsh monitors subrogation to ensure your interests protected
- Recovered funds may offset deductible

### Post-Claim Review

After major claims, Marsh conducts loss review:
- Root cause analysis
- Coverage performance evaluation
- Claim handling assessment
- Loss prevention recommendations
- Impact on renewal strategy

## Measuring Success

### Key Performance Indicators

Marsh tracks and reports on:
- **Total Cost of Risk**: Premiums + retained losses + risk management expenses
- **Claims Closure Rate**: Percentage of claims resolved within target timeframes
- **Premium Efficiency**: Cost per dollar of coverage (benchmarked against peers)
- **Service Response Times**: Certificate turnaround, inquiry response, endorsement processing
- **Market Access**: Number of competing quotes, carrier participation
- **Coverage Breadth**: Gaps identified and addressed, enhancements implemented
