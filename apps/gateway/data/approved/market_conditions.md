# Insurance Market Conditions & Industry Trends

*Last Updated: November 2025*

## Overview

The commercial insurance market is dynamic, with pricing and capacity fluctuating based on carrier performance, catastrophic events, economic conditions, and regulatory changes. Understanding current market conditions is essential for budget planning, renewal strategies, and coverage procurement.

**Disclaimer**: This document provides general market intelligence as of November 2025. Actual pricing and terms vary by industry, location, loss history, and individual risk characteristics. Consult with your Marsh broker for specific guidance.

---

## Current Market Summary (Q4 2025)

### Overall Market Conditions

**Market Characterization**: Transitional - Moving from hard market to moderate conditions

The commercial insurance market has been in a prolonged hard market cycle since 2019, but is showing signs of moderation in select lines. However, conditions remain challenging in catastrophe-exposed property and cyber liability.

**Key Drivers**:
- Persistent inflation affecting claims costs (loss adjustment, repair/replacement, medical costs)
- Increased catastrophic losses from climate events (hurricanes, wildfires, floods, severe convective storms)
- Rising cyber claims frequency and severity (ransomware, business interruption)
- Economic uncertainty impacting carrier investment returns
- Reinsurance cost increases passed through to primary market
- Social inflation driving liability settlements higher

## Rate Trends by Line of Business

### Property Insurance

**Current Rate Environment**: Hard Market (Moderating in some segments)

**Average Rate Changes**:
- Non-CAT zones: +5% to +15%
- CAT-exposed coastal: +15% to +40%
- Wildfire zones (Western US): +25% to +50%
- Flood-prone areas: +20% to +35%
- High-value properties: +10% to +25%

**Market Dynamics**:
- **Capacity**: Limited in catastrophe zones; carriers reducing aggregate limits in high-risk areas
- **Deductibles**: Increasing significantly in CAT zones (percentage deductibles common: 2-5% for wind/hail)
- **Valuation**: Carriers scrutinizing property values closely; inflation guard clauses standard
- **Coverage**: Sub-limits for equipment breakdown, ordinance & law, soft costs being imposed

**Industry-Specific Trends**:
- **Real Estate/Hospitality**: Coastal properties facing 30-50% increases; some accounts non-renewed
- **Manufacturing**: Moderating increases (5-10%) for non-CAT, well-protected facilities
- **Energy**: Wind-exposed Gulf Coast facilities seeing 40%+ increases
- **Healthcare**: Generally stable (5-15%) except CAT zones

**Outlook for 2026**: Continued pressure in catastrophe zones; potential for single-digit increases in favorable segments with strong risk management.

### Cyber Liability Insurance

**Current Rate Environment**: Hard Market (Severe)

**Average Rate Changes**:
- Healthcare: +25% to +50%
- Financial services: +20% to +40%
- Critical infrastructure: +30% to +60%
- Retail/e-commerce: +15% to +35%
- Small business (sub-$50M revenue): +10% to +25%
- Well-controlled risks with MFA/EDR: +5% to +15%

**Market Dynamics**:
- **Capacity**: Improved but selective; carriers requiring detailed security assessments
- **Underwriting**: Intense scrutiny on security controls (MFA, EDR, backups, patch management, security awareness training)
- **Retentions**: Increasing substantially (ransomware sub-limits common, 10-20% co-insurance for ransomware)
- **Coverage**: War/terrorism exclusions broadening; nation-state attack exclusions common
- **Applications**: Lengthier applications (30+ questions on security controls); supplemental questionnaires for tech sector

**Minimum Security Requirements** (most carriers now mandatory):
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) on all remote access and privileged accounts
- Endpoint Detection & Response (EDR) or equivalent on all endpoints
- Offline/immutable backups tested regularly
- Patch management program with documented process
- Security awareness training for all employees
- Incident response plan and vendor retainer
- Email security (anti-phishing, DMARC/DKIM/SPF)
- Privileged access management
- Network segmentation for critical systems

**Ransomware Coverage**: Most policies now include:
- Sublimits (50-80% of policy limit common)
- Co-insurance (insured retains 10-20% of ransomware loss)
- Higher retentions/deductibles for ransomware vs. other cyber events
- Proof of security controls before ransomware coverage granted

**Outlook for 2026**: Rate increases expected to moderate to +5-15% for well-controlled risks; continued pressure on under-controlled accounts. Security controls will remain key differentiator.

### General Liability (GL) & Umbrella/Excess

**Current Rate Environment**: Soft to Moderate

**Average Rate Changes**:
- General Liability: Flat to +5%
- Umbrella/Excess: +5% to +15%
- High-hazard operations: +10% to +25%
- Favorable risks: Flat to +3%

**Market Dynamics**:
- **Primary GL**: Relatively stable; competition among carriers
- **Umbrella/Excess**: More challenging; social inflation concerns driving nuclear verdicts (verdicts exceeding $10M)
- **Capacity**: Abundant for most risks; layered excess towers may require more markets
- **Underwriting**: Focus on loss history, hazard analysis, contractual risk transfer practices

**Social Inflation Impact**:
- Average liability settlement increased 30% over past 3 years
- "Nuclear verdicts" (>$10M) up 300% in past decade
- Third-party litigation funding increasing claim frequency and severity
- Reptile theory tactics in plaintiff litigation
- Concerns highest in: transportation, construction, products liability, premises liability

**Industry-Specific Trends**:
- **Construction**: Stable to +5% for well-managed contractors; focus on subcontractor management
- **Transportation**: +10-20% due to nuclear verdicts; extensive underwriting of safety programs
- **Hospitality**: Flat to +5%; liquor liability scrutinized
- **Manufacturing**: Products liability seeing +5-15%; increased focus on product safety/QC

**Outlook for 2026**: Continued moderate conditions for primary GL; umbrella/excess likely to see continued increases due to social inflation concerns.

### Workers Compensation

**Current Rate Environment**: Soft (Competitive)

**Average Rate Changes**: -5% to +5% (most accounts flat to slight decrease)

**Market Dynamics**:
- **Pricing**: Highly competitive; carriers seeking growth
- **Capacity**: Abundant across most industries
- **Experience Mod**: Remains critical pricing factor
- **State Variations**: Significant differences by state (e.g., California more expensive than many states)

**Key Pricing Factors**:
- Experience Modification Rate (EMR): Single biggest driver; 0.75 EMR can save 25% vs. standard rate
- Safety programs: Robust programs can earn 5-15% premium credits
- Claims management: Return-to-work programs, medical provider networks reduce costs
- Payroll classification: Accurate classification critical (shop vs. field, clerical separation)

**Industry-Specific Trends**:
- **Construction**: Competitive but varies by trade; focus on safety culture, subcontractor certificates
- **Healthcare**: Stable; back injuries and workplace violence remain concerns
- **Manufacturing**: Favorable market; automation reducing exposure
- **Hospitality**: Competitive; slip/fall and repetitive motion injuries focus areas

**Emerging Trends**:
- **Telecommuting**: Increased work-from-home reducing certain injury types but creating new exposures
- **Mental health claims**: Rising, particularly post-pandemic; some states expanding coverage
- **Opioid crisis**: Carriers scrutinizing medical management and pharmacy programs
- **Medical cost inflation**: 5-7% annually; offsetting frequency improvements

**Outlook for 2026**: Market expected to remain competitive with flat to slight decreases for accounts with good experience.

### Commercial Auto Liability

**Current Rate Environment**: Hard Market (Severe for Transportation)

**Average Rate Changes**:
- Light fleet (non-trucking): +5% to +15%
- Trucking/Transportation: +15% to +40%
- Delivery services: +10% to +25%
- Favorable fleets: Flat to +8%

**Market Dynamics**:
- **Nuclear verdicts**: Driving underwriting and pricing; verdicts >$10M up significantly
- **Distracted driving**: Cell phone use, DUI increasing frequency and severity
- **Capacity**: Limited for trucking, particularly long-haul; layered umbrella programs challenging
- **Telematics**: Increasingly required or incentivized; 10-15% discounts available

**Key Underwriting Factors**:
- MVR screening: Clean driver records essential; DUI typically declines coverage
- Safety programs: Dash cams, telematics, driver training can earn 15-25% credits
- Contractual risk transfer: Additional insured requirements from shippers/brokers
- FMCSA scores: Critical for trucking (CSA scores, safety ratings)
- Vehicle maintenance: Documented programs required

**Industry-Specific Trends**:
- **Trucking/Transportation**: Severely challenging; some carriers non-renewing, capacity scarce for higher-risk operations
- **Delivery services (last-mile)**: Gig economy drivers creating exposure; underwriting scrutiny increased
- **Construction**: Moderate increases; focus on driver selection and vehicle maintenance
- **Sales fleets**: Generally stable; clean fleets seeing flat renewals

**Outlook for 2026**: Continued pressure expected in transportation sector; moderate conditions for other fleets with strong safety programs.

## Alternative Risk Transfer & Capital Markets

### Captive Insurance

**Market Conditions**: Growing interest

Captive formation inquiries up 20% in 2025 vs. 2024, driven by:
- Hard market pricing in property and cyber
- Desire for more control over claims and risk financing
- Tax advantages (properly structured)
- Opportunity to access reinsurance market directly

**Popular Domiciles** (2025):
- Vermont (leading US domicile)
- Utah (cell captive leader)
- Delaware, Montana, Hawaii (growth states)
- Bermuda, Cayman Islands (offshore)

**Typical Candidates**:
- $25M+ in annual premium spend
- Strong risk management culture
- Predictable, controllable losses
- Long-term commitment (3-5 year minimum)

---

### Insurance-Linked Securities (ILS)

**Market Conditions**: Expanding

ILS capital providing alternative capacity, particularly for property catastrophe risk:
- Catastrophe bonds: $45B+ outstanding (2025)
- Collateralized reinsurance growing
- Sidecars and quota share structures

**Benefits for Buyers**:
- Additional capacity in hard markets
- Potentially lower cost vs. traditional reinsurance
- Multi-year terms (budget certainty)

---

### Parametric Insurance

**Market Conditions**: Emerging/Growing

Parametric policies (pay based on trigger event, not actual loss) growing for:
- Hurricane/windstorm (wind speed trigger)
- Earthquake (magnitude/location trigger)
- Business interruption (supply chain, weather, pandemic)
- Cyber (outage/downtime trigger)

**Advantages**:
- Fast payout (no loss adjustment)
- Covers uninsurable or hard-to-insure risks
- Complements traditional coverage

**2025 Adoption**: Still niche but growing 30%+ annually; most common for large corporates and catastrophe exposures.

## Strategies for Navigating Hard Market Conditions

### Risk Improvement Initiatives

Demonstrating risk improvement can offset market increases by 10-25%:

**Property**:
- Update building systems (electrical, plumbing, HVAC, roof)
- Install/upgrade fire protection (sprinklers, fire alarm)
- Implement IoT monitoring (water sensors, temp monitoring)
- CAT mitigation (hurricane shutters, roof strengthening, wildfire defensible space)

**Cyber**:
- Implement MFA on all remote access and privileged accounts
- Deploy EDR on all endpoints
- Establish offline/immutable backup with testing
- Conduct security awareness training quarterly
- Develop incident response plan and retain IR vendor
- Implement email security (DMARC, anti-phishing)

**Auto**:
- Install telematics/dash cams
- Enhance driver screening (MVR, drug testing)
- Implement driver training programs
- Establish vehicle maintenance protocols

**Workers Comp**:
- Develop return-to-work programs
- Implement safety training and toolbox talks
- Conduct ergonomic assessments
- Establish medical provider network

---

### Coverage & Retention Optimization

**Consider Higher Retentions/Deductibles**:
- Increasing deductibles by 50-100% can reduce premium 15-25%
- Self-insure more predictable, frequent losses
- Purchase insurance for catastrophic losses

**Example**: 
- Current: $10K deductible, $100K premium
- Optimized: $25K deductible, $80K premium
- Additional risk retained: $15K
- Premium savings: $20K
- Net benefit: $5K (plus investment income on savings)

**Coverage Restructuring**:
- Remove or reduce less critical coverages
- Tailor policy limits to actual exposures (avoid over-insurance)
- Consider aggregate deductibles for workers comp
- Evaluate policy buy-downs (reduce cyber ransomware sublimit to reduce premium)

---

### Multi-Year Agreements

In hard markets, locking in multi-year terms can provide budget certainty:
- **2-3 year policies**: Fix rates (or rate increase caps) for multiple years
- **Benefits**: Budget certainty, fewer renewals, relationship building with carriers
- **Considerations**: Less flexibility to change coverage; locked in even if market softens

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is a "hard market" vs. "soft market"?**

A: 
- **Hard Market**: Restricted capacity, rising prices, stricter underwriting, reduced coverage
- **Soft Market**: Abundant capacity, flat/decreasing prices, relaxed underwriting, expanded coverage

Markets are cyclical, typically lasting 3-7 years. We are currently transitioning from a hard market (2019-2025) to more moderate conditions in some lines.

**Q: Why do rates increase even if I haven't had claims?**

A: Insurance pricing reflects both individual risk characteristics and broader market conditions:
- **Loss trends**: Industry-wide loss experience affects all insureds
- **Reinsurance costs**: Carriers pass through increased reinsurance costs
- **Investment returns**: Poor carrier investment performance leads to underwriting pressure
- **Regulatory changes**: New regulations can increase carrier costs
- **Catastrophic events**: Major cat losses affect entire market

Even with excellent loss history, expect some increase in hard markets (though significantly less than peers with claims).

**Q: How much can risk improvements reduce my premium?**

A: Varies by line and improvement, but generally:
- **Cyber**: MFA + EDR + backups can reduce increases 15-30%
- **Property**: Sprinklers, updated systems can reduce increases 10-25%
- **Auto**: Telematics, driver training can reduce increases 15-25%
- **Workers Comp**: Safety programs, return-to-work can improve EMR 10-30% over 3 years

**Q: Should I increase deductibles to save premium?**

A: Depends on financial capacity and risk tolerance:
- **Higher deductibles**: Save 15-25% of premium but increase retained risk
- **Break-even analysis**: If claims frequency is low, savings typically exceed additional retained risk
- **Cash flow**: Ensure adequate liquidity to fund deductible payments
- **Recommendation**: Model expected losses at various deductible levels; consider aggregate deductibles for predictable risks (workers comp)

**Q: What is the best time to renew to get favorable pricing?**

A: Generally:
- **January 1**: Most competitive (most capacity, carrier budgets refresh)
- **April 1, July 1, October 1**: Standard quarterly renewals; good capacity
- **June 1** (hurricane season), **December 31** (year-end): Potentially more challenging

However, switching renewal dates just for pricing often isn't worth the effort. More important: Start renewal process early and provide quality submission information.

**Q: How long should I expect this hard market to last?**

A: Market cycles typically last 3-7 years. Current hard market began in 2019 and is showing signs of moderation in some lines (6 years). However, specific lines may differ:
- **Property**: CAT zones likely remain hard 2-3 more years (dependent on catastrophe activity)
- **Cyber**: Likely 1-2 more years of increases, then stabilization as controls improve
- **Casualty**: Social inflation is structural issue; may persist 3-5+ years

**Q: Can I negotiate policy wording in a hard market?**

A: Limited, but possible:
- **Priority**: Secure adequate limits and acceptable pricing first
- **Key terms**: Focus on critical terms (definitions, exclusions, sublimits) rather than minor wording
- **Leverage**: Best negotiated with favorable loss history, strong risk management, long-term relationship
- **Manuscript policies**: Available for large, sophisticated buyers but require significant premium

**Q: Should I consider a captive insurance company?**

A: Potentially, if:
- **Premium volume**: $500K+ annual premium (minimum); $2M+ ideal
- **Risk profile**: Predictable, controllable losses
- **Financial strength**: Strong balance sheet to capitalize captive ($250K-$1M+ required)
- **Commitment**: 3-5 year minimum time horizon
- **Expertise**: Work with experienced captive manager and consultant

Captives can provide 20-40% cost savings over 5+ years but require upfront investment and ongoing management.

## Glossary of Market Terms

**Capacity**: The maximum amount of insurance a carrier or market is willing to provide

**CAT (Catastrophe)**: Major natural disaster event (hurricane, earthquake, wildfire, etc.)

**Rate**: The price per unit of exposure (e.g., per $100 of property value, per $1,000 of payroll)

**Premium**: Total cost of insurance policy (rate × exposure)

**Retention/Deductible**: Amount insured retains before insurance pays

**Sub-limit**: Maximum amount payable for specific type of loss (less than overall policy limit)

**Co-insurance**: Percentage of loss insured retains (e.g., 20% co-insurance = insured pays 20% of covered loss)

**Aggregate Limit**: Maximum amount payable for all claims during policy period

**Per Occurrence Limit**: Maximum amount payable for any single claim/event

**Hard Market**: Insurance market characterized by rising prices, restricted capacity, stricter underwriting

**Soft Market**: Insurance market characterized by flat/decreasing prices, abundant capacity, relaxed underwriting

**Social Inflation**: Trend of increasing insurance claims costs driven by plaintiff attorney tactics, litigation funding, broader definition of liability, larger jury awards

**Nuclear Verdict**: Jury award exceeding $10 million

**Reinsurance**: Insurance purchased by insurance companies to transfer risk

**Facultative Reinsurance**: Reinsurance on individual policy

**Treaty Reinsurance**: Reinsurance covering book of business

**ILS (Insurance-Linked Securities)**: Investments (e.g., catastrophe bonds) that transfer insurance risk to capital markets

**Parametric Insurance**: Policy that pays based on trigger event (e.g., wind speed, earthquake magnitude) rather than actual loss

**Experience Modification Rate (EMR)**: Workers compensation rating factor based on loss history; 1.0 is average, <1.0 is better than average, >1.0 is worse

**Total Cost of Risk (TCOR)**: Premium + retained losses + risk management costs + cost of capital for retained risks
